Syria Narrative Tracker · Audience snapshot

What Syrian online audiences talk about, how they react, and how far they are from the media

11 September 2026 – 27 October 2026 · 46 days · 778 stories · 9,709 analyses of posts and comments by people and 4,870 by outlets

The Syria Narrative Tracker reads public posts and comments about Syria in Arabic, Kurdish and English several times a day (YouTube comments, Telegram channels, Bluesky, Reddit, X and news sites), groups them into stories and measures, story by story, the mood of ordinary people and the tone of each type of outlet. This snapshot draws audience indicators from that record. It describes what audiences say and do in public online; it does not represent all Syrians (see Method and limits).

Key findings

  1. Independent and private Syrian outlets are closest to the public mood. On the stories they covered, people's mood went the same way as their tone (negative, neutral or positive) 76% of the time, against 58% for Kurdish outlets. This is a proxy for whose framing audiences share, not a direct measure of trust.
  2. People are angrier than every type of outlet. Across all outlet types, people's reactions were clearly more negative than the coverage on 56% to 68% of the stories; outlets' tone was on average mostly neutral, people's leaning negative.
  3. Doubt is widespread. About 36% of people's attention went to stories where they voiced doubt about official accounts, promises or media reports, most of all on transitional justice (78%), north-east and the Kurdish question (55%), Israel and the south (41%).
  4. Coverage gaps. People talk more than outlets cover about society and daily life, north-east and the Kurdish question, governance and the new state, while outlets give more room than people's attention to international relations and economy and services.
  5. Signs of fatigue. Security and violence and governance and the new state lost a large part of people's attention in the second half of the period while outlets kept covering them. Tiredness, resignation or numbness marked 4% to 15% of people's attention from week to week.
  6. Video is where people react. YouTube comments make up 45% of the conversation analysed; reactions to governance and the new state and society and daily life happen mostly under videos (74% and 73%).
  7. Arabic and English audiences differ. Arabic reactions are leaning negative overall and centre on Israel and the south; English reactions are leaning negative and centre on north-east and the Kurdish question, and Kurdish reactions almost entirely on the north-east.
  8. Anger leads. The emotions most present in reactions are anger (15%), grief (6%), distrust (5%), fear (5%); hope appears in 5% of them and pride in 5%.

Method and limits

Source. Public posts and comments about Syria collected several times a day from YouTube comments, Telegram channels, Bluesky, Reddit, X and news sites, in Arabic, Kurdish and English. An AI model (Claude, by Anthropic) groups them into stories and rates the tone of people and of each outlet type, story by story; a validation tool compares its coding with human coding. Each story counts once a day (the day's last analysis).

Outlet types. State media (SANA, the Presidency, Al-Ikhbariya); independent and private Syrian outlets (Enab Baladi, Syria TV, Al-Watan and others); Arab and regional outlets (Al Jazeera, Al Arabiya, Asharq, TRT Arabi, Anadolu and others); Kurdish outlets (ANHA, North Press, Rudaw, Kurdistan24); international news through Google News.

Indicators. Doubt: reactions the model described as sceptical, suspicious, mocking or disbelieving of official accounts, promises or reports. Fatigue: a topic's share of people's attention falling by a quarter or more while outlets' share holds; tiredness: reactions carrying tiredness, resignation, numbness, fatalism or helplessness. Engagement: likes, views and comments drawn by the posts analysed, per post.

Limits. People who post and comment in public are not a sample of all Syrians: the data says nothing about age, gender, education or income, about people who are offline or do not post, or about private messaging (WhatsApp) and Facebook, which the tracker does not collect. Silence, news avoidance and self-censorship are invisible to it. Alignment with an outlet's tone is not trust. These indicators are meant to sit beside a representative survey, focus groups and interviews, which answer who audiences are, what they can access and why they trust what they trust.
