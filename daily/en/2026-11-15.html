The day's stories

  1. Car Bomb Kills Man and Girl in Jaramana Security and violence · People: strongly negative · Outlets: strongly negative · 21% of the discussion
    A bomb planted in a car exploded in Jaramana, Damascus countryside, on Saturday night, killing a man and a girl and wounding another person, according to Syria's Internal Security Forces. Security forces sealed the area and opened an investigation; the cause remains undetermined. International outlets linked it to a wave of attacks since Assad's fall.
  2. Government Cuts Diesel Price Again Amid Winter Preparations Economy and services · People: leaning negative · Outlets: leaning positive · 10% of the discussion
    Syria's pricing committee lowered first-grade diesel by about 14-15%, to 150 Syrian pounds a liter, effective midnight November 15, while keeping gasoline and gas prices unchanged. Officials framed it as easing living costs ahead of winter; outlets reported the move widely.
  3. Debate Over Government Performance and Corruption Dominates Online Discussion Governance and the new state · People: leaning negative · Outlets: mostly neutral · 9% of the discussion
    A YouTube segment asking 'who liberates, decides' sparked extensive debate about whether the transitional government, led by President Ahmad al-Shara, is delivering good governance. Commenters cited corruption, high prices, poor electricity, unemployment and alleged favoritism as evidence of failure, while some defended the leadership as having restored national dignity.
  4. Backlash Over TV Interview with Controversial Guest Omar Rahmoun Transitional justice · People: strongly negative · Outlets: mostly neutral · 7% of the discussion
    A televised interview featuring a guest called Omar Rahmoun, discussed in the context of transitional justice and former regime figures, triggered an avalanche of angry YouTube comments accusing the channel (described as 'al-Mashhad') of glorifying a former regime-linked figure. Commenters demanded accountability and criticized the channel's editorial judgment for hosting him.
  5. Homelessness Report in Damascus Sparks Debate Over Government Priorities Society and daily life · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 6% of the discussion
    A Syria TV report on families sleeping on Damascus streets due to a chronic housing and support crisis drew sharp public reaction. Commenters blamed government priorities, corruption and inadequate welfare programs, while others countered that homelessness exists in wealthy countries too and that recovery takes time.
  6. Israeli Forces Continue Incursions and Raids Across Quneitra and Daraa Israel and the south · People: leaning negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 4% of the discussion
    Israeli military aircraft and ground forces conducted multiple incursions in Daraa and Quneitra, including raiding homes in Jabata al-Khashab, firing on farmland in Wadi al-Raqqad, and shelling near al-Asbah. A Haaretz report, widely cited by Syrian outlets, quoted Israeli soldiers alleging the army fabricates pretexts to justify its continued presence.
  7. Electricity Rationing Worsens as Winter Approaches Economy and services · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 4% of the discussion
    Syrian state media attributed worsening electricity rationing to the Tishreen power plant going offline, fuel oil shortages, reduced Azerbaijani gas supplies, maintenance work, and illegal electricity theft. Public commenters in Damascus, Aleppo and elsewhere describe just 2-4 hours of power a day, blaming both corruption and the new government's failure to improve on Assad-era conditions.
  8. Mushroom and Water-Related Poisoning Outbreaks Kill Children in Raqqa and Hasakah Society and daily life · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 3% of the discussion
    Wild mushroom poisoning killed four children in rural Raqqa, with two more hospitalized in Aleppo, while Hasakah's Ras al-Ain reported nearly 290 cases of acute diarrhea and 35 of hepatitis A linked to suspect drinking water. Health authorities issued warnings and advisories as officials investigated causes.
  9. Syrian and Lebanese Interior Ministers Meet in Damascus International relations · Outlets: leaning positive · 3% of the discussion
    Syrian Interior Minister Anas Khattab received his Lebanese counterpart Ahmad al-Hajjar in Damascus on November 15 to discuss bilateral relations and security cooperation. Both state and independent outlets gave the visit heavy, largely celebratory coverage, framing it as supporting regional stability.
  10. Fire Destroys Syrian Refugee Camp Tents in Lebanon's Bekaa Valley Refugees and return · People: leaning negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 2% of the discussion
    A fire, reportedly caused by an electrical fault, burned around 30-70 tents and a car at a Syrian refugee camp in Bar Elias, Lebanon's Bekaa Valley, leaving close to 500 people homeless. The camp housed families originally from Raqqa and Aleppo; no serious injuries were reported.
  11. Lira Nears 15,000 to the Dollar Amid Economic Debate Economy and services · People: strongly negative · Outlets: mostly neutral · 2% of the discussion
    The Syrian pound weakened further, with the dollar reportedly reaching nearly 15,000 old lira (14,900-15,000) in Daraa and other markets on November 15, prompting criticism of the Central Bank governor and claims of internal disputes within the finance administration. The Central Bank issued its usual daily official bulletin.
  12. Lengthy Power Cuts Return to Damascus and Other Cities Economy and services · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 1% of the discussion
    Residents of Damascus, Aleppo and other cities report power cuts of up to 15 hours a day, which the state electricity company attributes to the Tishreen plant going offline, fuel shortages, rising global fuel prices, and reduced Azerbaijani gas supply. The issue drew heavy public frustration online.
  13. Government Settles MTN Dispute, Clears Way for Zain Entry Economy and services · People: leaning positive · Outlets: leaning positive · 1% of the discussion
    Syria's telecom regulator signed a final settlement with MTN on November 14, paving the way for Kuwait's Zain to take over the mobile network, expected to begin operating in the first quarter of 2027. Officials said talks began in February and denied reports of disputes with Zain over 5G equipment.
  14. Kurdish Students Continue Protests Over Restrictions on Kurdish-Language Education North-east and the Kurdish question · People: leaning negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 1% of the discussion
    Students in Kerî Legê and Rmeilan continue protesting a transitional Education Ministry decision limiting Kurdish-language instruction to three weekly periods, demanding the right to study in their mother tongue. Separately, Rmeilan oil field workers protested being transferred to US firm HKN on temporary contracts.
