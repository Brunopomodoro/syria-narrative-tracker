  • Former Porn Actress's Visit to Umayyad Mosque Sparks Public Controversy Spanish former adult-film actress Amarna Miller visited the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus and Palmyra wearing a hijab, accompanied by Syrian officials including culture ministry representatives, in mid-November. The visit, widely shared on social media and YouTube, triggered intense public backlash demanding the culture minister's resignation and questioning the vetting of foreign visitors.
  • Protests at Rojava University Over Kurdish Language and Academic Independence Students at Rojava University in Qamishli blocked a Syrian Ministry of Education delegation's vehicle on 16 November, demanding Kurdish-language education and academic independence after Damascus moved to affiliate some colleges with Euphrates University. Security forces used tear gas; the ministry says unlisted colleges won't be recognized, while the university's administration and Kobani students condemned the raid.
  • Israeli Incursions and Shooting in Daraa's Yarmouk Basin Villages Israeli forces repeatedly raided and opened fire in Kouya, Maariyeh, Abidin and al-Rafeed villages in western Daraa and southern Quneitra countryside on 16-17 November, searching homes, beating an elderly man, and prompting displacement. State and independent outlets reported the events almost in real time, while Syria's UN envoy Ibrahim Olabi told the Security Council Syrians are 'tired' of the attacks.
  • EU Foreign Ministers Agree to Advance Association Agreement With Syria EU foreign ministers meeting in Brussels on 16 November, led by Kaja Kallas, agreed to move forward with a 2008-drafted association agreement with Syria, aiming to build trade and political-dialogue frameworks after sanctions were lifted. Officials and EU commissioners framed Syria's stability as vital to regional security; Syrian state and independent media covered it widely.
  • Lira Continues Slide Past 14,900 to the Dollar Public comments and outlets note the Syrian pound continuing to weaken against the dollar, reaching around 14,900-16,000 in parts of the market, fueling renewed complaints about living costs. Some commenters blame government corruption and weak industry, while others defend the transitional government citing wartime legacy problems.

  • Remains of Six Found in Harasta, Mass Grave Count Rises to 28 Remains of six people were uncovered during rubble-clearing in Harasta's al-Ajami neighborhood in Rural Damascus, part of a project removing some 300,000 cubic meters of debris left by the former regime's bombardment. This brings the number of discovered mass graves in Syria since early 2026 to 28, according to Hawar News Agency.
  • Diesel Price Cut to 150 Lira Meets Mixed Reactions and Transit Fare Review Authorities cut diesel prices by 15% to around 150 lira, prompting the local administration minister to order governorates to review internal transport tariffs accordingly. Sawt al-Asima and other outlets polled public opinion; commenters questioned whether the cut is sufficient given still-high living costs.
