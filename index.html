What people are talking about · 26 October 2026, 19:57 UTC

Anxious Jordan's retaliatory strikes in southern Syria after the Karak church attack dominated coverage and public chatter, alongside a car bombing near Qastal that killed an al-Qaeda-linked commander and unexplained explosions in Daraa and Idlib. Attacks on Kurdish returnees to Tal Abyad continued to generate anger and sectarian framing differences between Kurdish and Arabic audiences. Economy news (Qatar investment talks, Berlin flights, ICAO lifting its warning) got positive but modest attention, while fuel shortages and diesel queues kept frustration simmering.

  • Jordan strikes alleged militant sites in southern Syria after Karak church attack Jordan's air force carried out 'Operation Jordanian Deterrence - Hammoud' against sites in southern Syria on 26 October, saying they were linked to the gunmen who attacked a funeral at Hammoud church in Karak the previous Friday. Jordanian officials said the strikes killed and wounded several fighters. Syrian state media and outlets widely republished the Jordanian statement, noting coordination with Damascus.
  • Education Ministry curriculum changes spark controversy over Ottoman history and women's representation New Syrian curricula removing anti-Ottoman references (like the 'Butcher of Arabs' label for Jamal Pasha) and altering depictions of women sparked debate online, with Turkish media framing it positively and some Syrian commenters criticizing both the content and the lack of transparency. The Education Ministry has defended the changes as reflecting Syrian diversity.
  • Attacks on Kurdish families returning to Tal Abyad/Kobani Kurdish families returning to Tal Abyad (Kobani countryside, Raqqa) faced physical attacks, death threats and car burnings on 26 October, according to Kurdish outlets and the Kurdish National Council, which demanded an investigation. Raqqa's internal security command said it is pursuing perpetrators, while over 100 families were evacuated to safety in Kobani.
  • Drone/car bombing kills al-Qaeda-linked commander near Qastal on Damascus-Homs highway A vehicle was struck on the Damascus-Homs highway near Qastal/Qara on 26 October, killing three people including Hurras al-Din commander Abu Abdulrahman al-Libi, according to local and international reports. Remnants matched a US AGM-114R9X Hellfire missile, suggesting a US strike possibly coordinated with the international coalition. SANA initially described it as a car explosion with an unclear source.
  • Suwayda-Damascus standoff continues amid US-Israel friction over Druze Suwayda remains in a standoff with Damascus, with reports of renewed clashes in the western countryside, drone attacks on checkpoints, and closure of the Suwayda-Damascus highway. A parliamentary delegation met Suwayda's governor to discuss dialogue, while US officials reportedly distinguished their position from Israel's on backing Druze forces.
  • Arrests of Assad's cousin and former regime officers over war crimes Syria's Interior Ministry announced the arrest of Yahya al-Assad, a cousin of ousted president Bashar al-Assad, along with several former regime officers in Homs, including figures linked to the 2013 Tadamon massacre, military pilots accused of bombing cities, and a terrorism court judge. Officials framed this as part of transitional justice efforts.
  • Landmine blasts continue to injure and kill children as map prepared Three children were injured by a landmine near al-Aboush village, Tal Tamr countryside north of Hasakah, on 26 October while herding sheep, North Press reported. Separately, a child was killed and his mother and uncle injured in an explosion in Hama countryside. Syria's Emergency Ministry said the country's first landmine contamination map will be released soon.

Across all three areas, formal institutional narratives of sovereignty, reform and transition sat uneasily against documented shortages, graft investigations and contested security operations on the ground.
