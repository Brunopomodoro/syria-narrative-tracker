Media coverage · Daily analysis · 28 October 2026

Jordan border reopens, Iraq's PM makes historic Damascus visit

Based on 105 reports and articles from 39 organisations.

In short: The media's dominant story was the aftermath of Jordan's deadly strikes on Daraa, with the reopening of the Nasib crossing and a joint Syrian-Jordanian investigative committee, alongside the first visit by an Iraqi prime minister to Damascus since Assad's fall. Coverage of the Daraa strikes ranged from Jordanian sources blaming a security vacuum to Syrian outlets questioning coordination and highlighting civilian deaths, while the Iraq visit drew extensive attention to oil, trade and security cooperation. Economic coverage focused on currency instability and Syria's $22 billion debt burden even as officials touted banking sector revival.

Security

The media gave heavy coverage to the fallout from Jordan's strikes on Daraa that killed civilians, with Asharq Al-Awsat reporting Syria held funerals for victims while Jordan expressed regret, and Al Jazeera, Qatar-funded, reporting the border reopened amid a probe. A joint Syrian-Jordanian security committee was formed, according to The National, The Jerusalem Post and Anadolu Agency, Turkish state-run, to investigate the strikes, with Enab Baladi, an independent Syrian outlet, and Hawar News, affiliated with the Kurdish-led administration, confirming the committee's formation. The pro-Israel Foundation for Defense of Democracies argued the strikes exposed a security vacuum in southern Syria, while a Jordanian source cited by Asharq Al-Awsat said areas outside government control there are a haven for terrorists and drug traffickers; by contrast, Zaman al-Wasl, independent, reported that Jordanian journalists and their government failed to provide evidence of the alleged terrorists, and Syria TV, independent, questioned who identified the targets. Separately, Syria TV and Zaman al-Wasl reported continued Israeli incursions into Quneitra, including an abduction and new checkpoint, while Xinhua reported Israeli bulldozing destroyed olive groves in southern Syria. Enab Baladi and The Syrian Observer reported a French fighter killed in clashes with Syrian security forces in Idlib, and Anadolu Agency reported two Defense Ministry personnel killed by unidentified gunmen in Idlib.

Economy

Economic coverage centred on the Nasib border crossing's reopening, reported by Syria TV, independent, and Al-Quds Al-Arabi, after its closure following the strikes. Al-Watan Online, independent, cited an expert saying the dollar trading at 20,000 in merchants' books reflects an unclear monetary policy entrenching a cash market, while Enab Baladi reported Syria remains burdened with debts from the previous era exceeding 22 billion dollars. L'Orient-Le Jour similarly reported the Syrian pound under pressure with dollars kept out of banks. Against this, Syria TV examined whether Syria's banking sector is reviving amid moves toward Visa and Mastercard access and a $100 million initiative, and Hawar News, affiliated with the Kurdish-led administration, reported that lack of support and rising prices are pushing Jazira farmers to cut cultivated areas. The Iraqi PM's visit also carried economic weight, with The New Region and Shafaq News reporting discussion of oil exports through Baniyas, and L'Orient-Le Jour noting economic matters dominated the visit.

Governance

Iraqi PM Ali al-Zaidi's first visit to Damascus since Assad's fall dominated governance coverage, with The National and TRT World, Turkish state-linked, calling it a high-profile first trip, and Syria TV, independent, and Al-Watan Online reporting his meeting with President al-Sharaa. Compared with what people said

Ordinary Syrians' reactions to the Daraa strikes were far angrier than the media's measured diplomatic framing, with posts reaching up to 91% of discussion and sharply negative tone, including widespread mockery of Jordanian officials and accusations of collusion with Israel, a framing the media largely avoided. The Iraqi PM's visit drew milder, mixed public reaction (up to 22% of posts, mildly positive), with some welcoming it and others criticizing a reported donation to religious sites as out of touch given poverty, a detail absent from the media coverage reviewed. Syrians also discussed topics barely touched by the media sampled here: angry Turkish and Kurdish-language backlash over the SDF's integration deal with Damascus and attacks on Kurdish returnees in Tal Abyad, controversy over university mergers affecting Kurdish institutions, reactions to Israel's tight election, and flash floods in Raqqa, Hasakah and Deir ez-Zor.
