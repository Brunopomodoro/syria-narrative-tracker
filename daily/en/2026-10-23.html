Nationwide protests over Israeli incursions dominate coverage

Syria's justice minister, cited by SANA and Syria TV, called the protests a legitimate popular and legal response, while Syria Observatory for Human Rights (a UK-based monitor, London-registered) and Etana detailed a surge in Israeli ground operations, with Etana recording 3,200 such operations in the demilitarized zone since December 2024. Multiple outlets, including Syria TV, Zaman al-Wasl, SANA and Anadolu Agency (Turkish state-run), reported Israeli incursions, shelling and road closures in Quneitra countryside, with SANA and Syria TV reporting the arrest of a shepherd near Hader. The UN's deputy special envoy, cited by Syria TV and North Press, called on Israel to respect Syrian sovereignty. Separately, Syria TV, Zaman al-Wasl and Al-Modon covered the Rankous security operation against an alleged Islamic State cell, with Syria's interior ministry citing precise intelligence, while Enab Baladi reported local protests demanding government clarifications. Commentary pieces, including Kommersant and The New Arab, speculated about an Israeli agenda behind unrest in Sweida.

Economy

Economic coverage highlighted mounting hardship despite reforms. Syria Direct reported that ordinary Syrians continue to struggle with rising living costs even as the country reconnects to global financial markets. Syria TV detailed a worsening diesel shortage in Latakia threatening transport, agriculture and heating, while Al-Araby Al-Jadeed (Qatar-funded) reported attacks on power lines disrupting daily life and Asharq Al-Awsat (Saudi-owned) reported three power stations went offline after a gas pipeline explosion. Al-Jumhuriya reported delayed wheat payments freezing markets in the Jazira region, and Al-Araby Al-Jadeed reported Hasakah farmers facing tripled prices from field to market. On a more positive note, SANA reported nearly 17,000 new business registrations over nine months, and Syria TV reported road rehabilitation contracts awarded for three strategic routes. Al-Watan Online examined whether subsidies could ease living costs, while Syria TV investigated groundwater depletion from unregulated well-digging in Aleppo.

Governance

Governance coverage centered on diplomacy and state-building debates. Syria's foreign ministry condemned the attack on a church in Jordan's Karak, reported by SANA, Al-Watan Online, Syria TV and North Press. Syria TV and Zaman al-Wasl reported Syrian-Turkish talks on emergency cooperation and a joint emergency room. US envoy Tom Barrack, cited by Syria TV and Al-Watan Online, said a strike on Iran was possible and denied US support for Druze figures, while Al-Modon reported Barrack saying Syria is avoiding an escalation trap. Syria's UN envoy Olabi, per SANA and Syria TV, called for protecting sovereignty and warned of a new migration crisis tied to the "Syria without camps" vision. Syria TV (independent) analyzed how Assad's fall has not ended the "Assadist mentality" in state-building, and North Press (affiliated with the Kurdish-led administration) reported the Kurdistani Yekiti Party's congress held without most of its leadership. Al Akhbar reported Damascus reconsidering relations with Alawites amid talk of administrative decentralisation on the coast.
