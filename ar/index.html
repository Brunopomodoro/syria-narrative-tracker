What people are talking about · 29 October 2026, 05:39 UTC

Divided Coverage this cycle centers on the Iraqi PM's historic Damascus visit, widely welcomed but also mocked by some as self-serving; severe floods across Raqqa, Hasakah and Deir ez-Zor dominating emergency news; continued anger over Jordan's strikes on Daraa and Israeli incursions in Quneitra; and north-east tensions over university integration and curriculum changes. Public reaction splits between hope for Arab unity, skepticism toward officials, and weary complaints about economy and services.

  • Iraqi PM Ali al-Zaidi makes historic first visit to Damascus Iraqi PM Ali al-Zaidi visited Damascus on 28 October 2026, meeting President Ahmed al-Sharaa to discuss oil exports via the Baniyas pipeline (up to 2 million barrels daily), trade and security cooperation. Zaidi donated $10 million split between the Sayyida Zaynab shrine and Umayyad Mosque. It is the first such visit since Assad's fall.
  • Fallout from Jordanian airstrikes on Daraa continues Syria summoned Jordan's ambassador on 28 October over Jordanian airstrikes on Daraa that killed several civilians, including children, which Jordan linked to a church attack in Kerak. The two countries agreed to form a joint security committee, and the Nasib border crossing reopened after a brief closure.
  • Shop closures and layoffs spark debate over Damascus and Aleppo market stagnation Reports on shop closures and worker layoffs in Damascus and Aleppo markets drew heavy public commentary blaming high rents, Turkish and Chinese import competition, corruption and poor governance. Commenters compared current hardship unfavorably to, and in some cases nostalgically with, the Assad era.
  • Heavy rains and flash floods hit Raqqa, Hasakah, Deir ez-Zor Heavy rainfall and flash floods struck Raqqa, Hasakah, Deir ez-Zor and other provinces on 28-29 October 2026, prompting school and university closures, road and bridge closures, and emergency rescues. The Emergency Ministry reported over 250 distress calls, 15 civilians rescued and 375 families evacuated; at least two deaths were recorded.
  • Backlash over Rojava and Kobani university mergers and SDF integration Syria's Higher Education Council decided to merge Rojava University with Euphrates University and Kobani University with Aleppo University, provoking protests from students and academics who say it erases Kurdish identity. Separately, returning displaced Kurds to Tel Abyad faced attacks, and debate continues over integrating YPJ into the Interior Ministry.
  • Concerns over gender representation in revised school curricula The fact-checking platform Ta'akad documented deletions of images of women and girls, added hijabs to illustrations, and removed lessons on citizenship and political participation in new Syrian textbooks compared to older editions. Women's rights groups like Femina called on the Education Ministry to reverse the changes.
  • Israeli incursions and shelling continue in Quneitra Israeli forces conducted multiple incursions into Quneitra countryside on 28 October, detaining and releasing a young man near Abu Mathra village, shelling farmland near Saida al-Jolan, and entering Jabata al-Khashab. Outlets tracked these as part of an ongoing pattern of near-daily incursions into southern Syria.
  • Clash near French Camp in Idlib kills French foreign fighter, two soldiers assassinated Internal Security forces killed a French foreign fighter known as Abu Ayman al-Faransi near Harem, Idlib, on 28 October after clashes with the 'French Camp' group. Hours later, two Defense Ministry personnel were shot dead by unknown gunmen in al-Fu'ah, raising fears of retaliation.
  • Arrests made in killing of driver Qais Sanufa near Damascus Internal Security arrested four suspects in Qudsaya, rural Damascus, for allegedly luring and killing driver Qais Sanufa to rob him; his body was found in Wadi Basima after he was reported missing. The case was first reported around a week ago and arrests were announced this cycle.

Economic coverage focused on currency instability and Syria's $22 billion debt burden even as officials touted banking sector revival.
  • Incursions, Reform Pledges and Unresolved Property Rights This week's media coverage converged on expanding Israeli military activity across southern Syria, a cluster of international financing deals presented as a turning point for recovery, and a cabinet reshuffle alongside unresolved property and minority-rights questions. Across all three areas, formal institutional narratives of sovereignty, reform and transition sat uneasily against documented shortages, graft investigations and contested security operations on the ground.
