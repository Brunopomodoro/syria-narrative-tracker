  1. Israeli Incursions and Shooting in Daraa's Yarmouk Basin Villages Israel and the south · People: strongly negative · Outlets: strongly negative · 20% of the discussion
    Israeli forces repeatedly entered the villages of Kuya and Maariya in western Daraa, firing on homes and farmland, searching houses, assaulting a minibus driver and an elderly man, and shelling the area, causing house fires and displacement. Local, state and international outlets, plus a UN envoy, documented the raids as part of ongoing operations described as violating Syrian sovereignty.
  2. EU Foreign Ministers Agree to Advance Association Agreement; Syria Partnership Forum in Damascus International relations · People: mostly neutral · Outlets: leaning positive · 18% of the discussion
    EU foreign ministers backed reviving a 2008 association agreement with Syria, with Kaja Kallas urging Syria drop recognition of occupied Georgian and Ukrainian territory. Simultaneously, the Syria Partnership Coordination Forum opened in Damascus with FM Shaibani, EU Commissioner Šuica and World Bank officials, announcing a €175m EU package and a $500m World Bank grant. Officials framed this as Syria's reintegration into global finance after Assad's fall.
  3. Former Porn Actress's Visit to Umayyad Mosque Sparks Public Controversy Society and daily life · People: leaning negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 13% of the discussion
    A visit by Spanish former adult-film actress Amarna Miller to the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus and to Palmyra sparked widespread debate online, with some outlets alleging the trip received government backing. Many Syrians criticized the Ministry of Culture for hosting her, while others defended her right to visit as a tourist.
  4. Protests at Rojava University Over Integration and Kurdish-Language Education North-east and the Kurdish question · People: mostly neutral · Outlets: leaning negative · 12% of the discussion
    Students and staff at Rojava University in Qamishli protested against government plans to affiliate the university with Euphrates University, demanding recognition of its academic independence and Kurdish-language instruction. A government delegation visiting the campus was confronted and security forces reportedly used tear gas, drawing condemnation from the Autonomous Administration, SDC and civil groups.
  5. Lira Slide and Old-Currency Exchange Crisis in Hasakah and Qamishli Economy and services · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 3% of the discussion
    The Syrian lira continued its slide against the dollar, while in Hasakah and Qamishli old banknotes lost nearly all value as Central Bank limits on exchange (one request per two weeks, capped at 10 million pounds) caused the Hal market to close for three straight days in protest. Money changers and residents described growing hardship from the currency transition.
  6. Saudi Firm Wins Contract to Rehabilitate Damascus-Deir ez-Zor Highway Economy and services · Outlets: leaning positive · 2% of the discussion
    Transport Minister Yarub Badr signed the first contract to oversee rehabilitation of the Damascus-Deir ez-Zor road and build a new 426km second lane, costing around $320 million over 800 days, with Saudi firm SETS supervising. The Islamic Development Bank is funding a related $2.9 million study for two new strategic transport corridors linking Jordan, Turkey and Iraq.
  7. Damascus Trial of Assad-Era Officials; Mass Grave Remains Found in Harasta Transitional justice · People: mostly neutral · Outlets: leaning positive · 2% of the discussion
    A Damascus transitional justice court opened trial of 24 former regime officials, including Bashar al-Assad and Ali Mamlouk tried in absentia, on charges of killing, torture and enforced disappearance; the session was adjourned to December 1. Separately, remains of six people were found in Harasta, raising Syria's documented mass-grave count to 28 since early 2026.
  8. Diesel Price Cut Fails to Lower Transit Fares, Deepening Public Frustration Economy and services · People: strongly negative · Outlets: mostly neutral · 2% of the discussion
    Despite a 14% cut in diesel prices (from 175 to 150 new Syrian pounds per liter), minibus and transport fares in Damascus and Aleppo remained unchanged, prompting the Local Administration Ministry to order governorates to review internal transit tariffs. Commuters and transport officials voiced skepticism that any fare relief would materialize quickly.
  9. Unexplained Illness Cases With Swelling Symptoms Reported in Several Provinces Society and daily life · Outlets: mostly neutral · 1% of the discussion
    Syria's Health Ministry said it is monitoring patients in Damascus countryside, Deir ez-Zor and Aleppo showing similar symptoms including swelling of the eyelids and cheeks and yellowing skin, with cases also reported in Hajin, Deir ez-Zor countryside. The ministry said patients are stable and found no sign so far of an infectious disease, while investigations continue.
  10. Life Sentence Issued in Matouna Village Civilian Killings in Suwayda Suwayda and the Druze · Outlets: leaning positive · 1% of the discussion
    A military criminal court in Damascus sentenced a member of internal security forces, Izz al-Din Ibrahim Abdul Aziz, to life in prison for killing civilians in the village of Matouna in northern Suwayda countryside on 7 February 2026. The Suwayda media directorate announced the verdict, which outlets framed as a rare accountability step tied to the Suwayda violence earlier in the year.
  11. Syrian Authorities Foil Major Captagon Smuggling Attempt From Lebanon Security and violence · Outlets: leaning positive · 1% of the discussion
    Syria's Anti-Narcotics Directorate thwarted an attempt to smuggle 261,000 Captagon pills from Lebanon across the border, arresting one suspect. The seizure follows other recent interceptions, amid continued Syrian-Lebanese efforts against cross-border drug trafficking.
  12. Remains of Six Found in Harasta, Mass Grave Count Rises to 28 Transitional justice · Outlets: leaning negative · 0% of the discussion
    Remains of six people were discovered in Harasta, Rif Dimashq, during rubble clearance, raising the number of mass graves uncovered in Syria since early 2026 to 28. Kurdish outlet ANHA reported the find as part of ongoing transitional justice efforts.
