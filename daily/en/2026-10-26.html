1. The day at a glance

Angry The dominant story overnight was Jordan's retaliatory airstrikes on southern Syria after the Karak church attack, which killed civilians in Daraa and triggered a border closure and Syrian government protest. Public reaction split sharply between Syrians condemning Jordan for killing women and children, Jordanians defending the strikes, and many invoking sectarian or geopolitical conspiracy theories. Other threads included continued fallout from attacks on Kurdish returnees in Tal Abyad, a drone strike killing an al-Qaeda-linked commander near Qastal, arrests of former regime officials, and controversy over new school curricula and Druze politics.

3. What the media said

Jordanian strikes on south Syria dominate a tense news day

In short: Jordanian airstrikes on southern Syria after the Karak church attack, and a car bombing near Qastal targeting an alleged Hurras al-Din figure, dominated security coverage, while Syrian, Qatari and Bahraini outlets reported a flurry of economic diplomacy. Governance coverage focused on curriculum controversy, Kurdish-language education demands and transitional justice efforts, while ordinary Syrians were far angrier about the Jordanian strikes and a worsening diesel crisis that went largely unmentioned by international media. The lifting of ICAO's 13-year warning on Syrian airspace was widely covered as a symbolic milestone.

Security

Jordan's army launched what it called 'Operation Jordanian Deterrence, Hammoud' against sites in southern Syria on 26 October, saying the targets were linked to gunmen behind an attack on a church in Karak, according to North Press and ANHA, a Kurdish-administration-affiliated outlet. SANA, Syria's state-run news agency, carried the government's official statement, while its foreign ministry said it had told Jordan it objected to operations on Syrian territory without prior coordination, per SANA and Al-Watan Online. SANA reported the death toll from the strikes in Daraa countryside rose to seven killed and three wounded, while France 24 said the strikes killed several civilians; the Nassib border crossing was closed, per SANA. Separately, Syria TV reported the international coalition targeted a figure known as Abu Abdul Rahman al-Libi in Qara, Rif Dimashq, amid a car bomb explosion on the Damascus-Homs highway that TASS said killed three people; Al-Modon, an independent Lebanese outlet, linked the killing to a Hurras al-Din leader. The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights, a UK-based monitor, reported heavy Israeli aerial activity over southern Syria (Syrian Observatory for Human Rights), while Anadolu Agency, Turkish state-run, said Israel is extending a military barrier through farmland there. Elsewhere, SANA and The New Arab reported the arrest of senior former Assad-regime officials including a cousin of Bashar al-Assad, and Syrian and international outlets widely covered ICAO lifting its 13-year airspace warning (Enab Baladi, TRT World).

Syrian and regional outlets reported a wave of economic diplomacy, with SANA reporting Syria and Bahrain moving toward banking cooperation, and SANA and Gulf Times covering Syria-Qatar trade and investment talks in Doha, where SANA said Syria is seeking investment partnerships at the World Investment Forum. Zaman al-Wasl reported nine international contracts to rehabilitate Syrian roads and link Iraq to Mediterranean ports, while Zaman al-Wasl said Syria allocated $643 million for roads and railways. Enab Baladi reported the Syrian pound's decline is worsening inflationary pressures, with an expert proposing an early-warning indicator. Enab Baladi and Enab Baladi reported Turkey lowered its forecast for Syrian refugee returns in 2026, and Al-Jumhuriya, an independent outlet, said delayed wheat payments have frozen markets in the Jazira region. Hawar News, a Kurdish-administration-affiliated outlet, reported sunflower farmers in the north-east facing rising costs and trader control over prices, and Enab Baladi said Israeli incursions threaten Quneitra farmers' livelihoods.

Syrian outlets reported controversy over school curriculum images, with Syria TV and ANHA saying the Education Ministry defended changes as reflecting Syrian society's diversity, and Hawar News noting the education authority's comments on the nature of the revisions. Rudaw, a Kurdish outlet, reported a senior politician saying Kurdish-language education must be enshrined in Syria's constitution, echoed by Hawar News calling for unified political efforts to protect Kurdish language rights, and ANHA quoting Ilham Ahmed saying Kurds in Syria have affirmed their existence.
