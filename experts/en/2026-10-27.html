Media coverage · Daily analysis · 27 October 2026

Jordan's Daraa strikes dominate as Damascus seeks joint probe

Based on 120 reports and articles from 38 organisations.

Share:Download PDFXFacebookTelegramWhatsApp

In short: Coverage on 27 October was overwhelmingly consumed by Jordan's airstrikes on Daraa and Sweida countryside, which Amman described as retaliation for a deadly church attack in Karak. Syrian outlets reported civilian deaths including children and the elderly, Damascus's denial of any prior coordination, and the swift move toward a joint security committee, while international media relayed Jordan's own death tolls and justifications. Economic reporting, far smaller in volume, highlighted company registrations, refinery rehabilitation and the reopening of the Nassib crossing.

Security

The dominant story was Jordan's airstrikes on sites in Daraa and Sweida countryside, which Amman called 'Jordanian Deterrence - Hamoud' in retaliation for a church attack in Karak. SANA reported emergency search and rescue operations, while Syria TV said victims included children, an elderly woman and an octogenarian. The independent Enab Baladi and Zaman al-Wasl reported Damascus denying any prior coordination, and Al-Watan Online said Syria formally registered its reservation with Jordan. International outlets gave varying tolls: Al Jazeera, funded by Qatar, cited at least seven dead, Le Monde said at least six, and Turkish state-run Anadolu Ajansı said five civilians were killed, while Jordan told the BBC and Arab News its strikes killed more than 20 Daesh militants. Syria closed the Nassib border crossing, reported by Syria TV and Chinese state-run Xinhua, and Syrian commentary, including a Syrian Bar Association statement in Syria TV and a researcher cited by Al-Watan Online, questioned Jordan's justification and demanded independent investigation. By the end of the day, SANA and Al-Watan Online reported Syria and Jordan had agreed to form a joint security committee. Separately, Syria TV and Enab Baladi reported continuing Israeli incursions and detentions in Quneitra countryside.

Economy

Economic coverage was lighter and mostly came from the independent think tank The Syria Report. It found Damascus dominates new company registrations while Saudi firms lead foreign branch openings, and interviewed the Companies Directorate head on regulatory reform and digital transformation. It also reported on Syria's emerging AI sector amid infrastructure challenges, and said Syria is rehabilitating the Homs and Banias refineries while the Al-Furqlus project contract remains unawarded. The independent Enab Baladi examined what opportunities 50 million cubic metres of war rubble could offer for reconstruction, and Al-Watan Online reported the reopening of the Busayri-Tanf road as a regional trade corridor, while SANA said Saudi Arabia approved a memorandum on municipal and housing cooperation with Syria.

Governance

Governance coverage centered on the political response to the Jordan crisis. Enab Baladi reported that Syria's People's Assembly condemned the Jordanian bombing while the Constitutional Committee called it a war crime, and SANA said the assembly offered condolences to victims' families. Al-Watan Online framed Damascus's posture as a measured assertion of sovereignty aimed at preserving ties with Jordan, while Hawar News, affiliated with the Kurdish-led administration, reported the foreign ministry's condemnation and plan for a joint field and security committee. Separately, SANA and Syria TV reported President al-Sharaa saying Syria and Iraq face a historic opportunity to build strong states, calling the economy a gateway for partnership. The independent Zaman al-Wasl reported the health ministry threatened legal action against it over an investigation into spoiled medicines, and separately exposed fraud that led to a hospital director's dismissal.

Compared with what people said

Ordinary Syrians' discussion matched the media's focus almost exactly: the Jordanian strikes on Daraa and Sweida made up as much as 96% of people's posts tracked that day, with reactions far angrier than the media's own tone, averaging strongly negative, full of grief, demands for accountability and compensation, and rejection of Jordan's justification. A second story largely absent from the media items reviewed here was attacks on Kurdish families returning to Tal Abyad, which Kurdish-language posters discussed with anger as part of a broader pattern of insecurity for returnees, a theme not covered in the day's media selection. A smaller share of posts also debated the Education Ministry's curriculum changes around Ottoman history, a topic that did appear in the media (noted by Turkish outlet Hürriyet), though people's tone on it was only mildly negative compared to the fury over the strikes.
