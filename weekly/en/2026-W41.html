The week's discussion was dominated by two intertwined currents of anguish: continued Israeli incursions into Quneitra and Daraa, and a worsening economic picture as the Syrian pound slid past 140 to the dollar. Israel-south stories drew the most sustained attention across all 30 updates, with people's tone at a stark -0.50, reflecting anger and a sense of helplessness over the UN's report of 280 incursions in a single month and 49 Syrians held. The economy theme, while a smaller share of volume, carried the weakest public mood of the week (-0.60), as contaminated fuel, currency chaos and perceived official indifference fed widespread mockery and despair.

North-east and Kurdish-related stories grew in share and carried some of the sharpest language divides of the week. Arabic-language commenters in Hasakah generally favored integration with Damascus and viewed Saddam-era posters as provocative, while Kurdish-language commenters expressed deep distrust of both Damascus and SDF leadership, several invoking fears of assimilation and Turkish interference. A currency-exchange deadline confusion in Hasakah, amplified by a money-transfer company's conflicting claims, triggered a dollar-rate spike and showed low-level signs of coordinated posting (burst patterns) worth watching rather than concluding.

International relations rose sharply in share, driven by unconfirmed reports of Syrian troop deployment to Yemen (which officials denied) and the Houthi attack on Riyadh's airport that killed Syrians among the dead, drawing grief and regional solidarity. Diplomatic contacts, Sharaa's outreach to Saudi Arabia and the UAE, and the foreign minister's talks with US and Turkish officials, were covered far more warmly by outlets (+0.08 to +0.30) than by the public, who remained skeptical of Syria's independence in these dealings.

A lighter, more hopeful thread ran through society and economy stories: the reopening of Aleppo's Great Umayyad Mosque after 13 years drew pride (+0.30 people, +0.50 outlets), even as some English-language comments raised sectarian blame questions. Investment announcements (Eagle Hills, Syrian Airlines rebrand, tech-sector diplomacy with Meta and Microsoft) produced cautious optimism, though consistently tempered by skepticism that benefits will reach ordinary citizens amid the currency crisis. The relocation of the Saladin statue in Damascus split opinion sharply, spawning conspiracy theories linking it to Turkish, Kurdish or Israeli influence.

Overall tone stayed flat week-on-week for the public (-0.20) while outlets held near neutral (+0.00), masking a growing gap between official framing of 'progress' stories (security arrests, diplomacy, mosque reopening) and public anger over daily economic hardship and foreign incursions. The sharpest single-day dip came October 11 (-0.31), coinciding with a surge in volume and renewed Saladin statue controversy.
