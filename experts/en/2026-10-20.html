Israeli incursions persist in Quneitra and Sweida as Damascus pursues reconstruction deals and new appointments

Based on 120 reports and articles from 37 organisations.

Share:XFacebookTelegramWhatsApp

In short: Israeli forces continued incursions and detentions in Quneitra and launched drone strikes on security posts in Sweida, while Washington's envoy publicly urged Israel to halt its operations, according to Syria TV and North Press. On the economic front, The Syria Report notes the WB-IMF meetings concluded with a trust fund and $406 million in Saudi lending, alongside a reshuffle of Syria's Petroleum Company leadership amid a gasoline scandal. Governance saw a cluster of new appointments, including an industry minister and sovereign fund chairman, covered by The Syria Report and Syria TV.

Security

Israeli forces carried out multiple incursions into Quneitra countryside, cutting roads and detaining and later releasing young men, according to Al-Watan Online, Enab Baladi and the Turkish state-run Anadolu Agency. West of Sweida, armed groups attacked Internal Security posts with explosive-laden drones, Syria TV reports. US envoy Tom Barrack called for an end to Israeli military operations in Syria and said Washington seeks a security agreement, per North Press and Syria TV; the pro-Israel Times of Israel and Al-Monitor separately reported Turkey's defence minister saying Turkish troops will remain in Syria and Iraq until border security is ensured. The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights documented 36 extrajudicial killings since early 2026 over alleged links to the Assad regime, and reported four SDF brigades completed integration training with the Defence Ministry (Syrian Observatory for Human Rights, Syrian Observatory for Human Rights). Hawar News, affiliated with the Kurdish-led Autonomous Administration, and ANHA examined whether ISIS still poses a threat nine years after Raqqa's liberation.

Economy

The WB-IMF annual meetings concluded with a multi-donor trust fund and $406 million in Saudi concessional lending for reconstruction, The Syria Report says, while SANA describes the START Fund as a new chapter in reconstruction and cites economists calling it a pillar for sustainable development (SANA, SANA). Syria's Petroleum Company leadership was overhauled amid a gasoline scandal and ongoing oil contract investigations, per The Syria Report, even as the first Saudi Aramco diesel shipment arrived at Baniyas and JPMorgan facilitated a Syrian letter of credit for Saudi oil products, Reuters and SANA report. Experts estimate bringing Syria's anti-money laundering regime to international standards could take three to five years, The Syria Report notes. Syria's postal service resumed international parcel shipping after over a decade despite chronic deficits, per The Syria Report, and the government raised water prices while cost-of-living estimates for a family reached 13 million liras, Al-Watan Online reports. Deir Ezzor's international airport still lacks basic facilities months after opening, the Syrian Observatory for Human Rights says (Syrian Observatory for Human Rights).

Governance

President al-Sharaa appointed Bassel AbdulAziz AbdulHanan as Minister of Industry and Omar Al-Halabi, a Saudi-based technocrat, as Economic Adviser and sovereign fund chairman, The Syria Report and The Syria Report report; Syria TV analyses the significance of the reshuffle across industry, oil and the sovereign fund (Syria TV). The independent outlet Syria TV examines residents' views of dialogue calls in Sweida and unresolved property rights disputes over expropriated land (Syria TV, Syria TV). North Press reports conflicting statements and defection accusations in the Kurdistani Yekiti party ahead of its tenth congress. The European Union welcomed continued Syrian cooperation with the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons, per North Press, and a judge was sentenced to prison and stripped of civil rights for serious violations, Halab Today reports.

Compared with what people said

Ordinary Syrians' top concern, fear of a Druze offensive against government forces in Sweida tied to alleged Israeli backing, drew sharp sectarian-divided reactions (-0.46 tone) that go well beyond the media's more factual reporting on dialogue calls and drone strikes covered by Syria TV and Syria TV. People also voiced strong anger over prices and exchange rate volatility (-0.53), a theme the media touched only indirectly through Al-Watan Online's cost-of-living figures. Posters mocked Erdogan's rhetoric about Israel sowing chaos as hollow, a more skeptical tone than the straightforward coverage of Turkey's troop-presence statement by outlets like Daily Sabah. A basketball match controversy over chants about a revolution-era figure sparked nationwide protests and hashtags, a story entirely absent from the day's media and expert reporting. Public reaction to the new WB trust fund was cautiously skeptical about corruption and debt, contrasting with the more upbeat framing in SANA's report citing economists.
