Media coverage · Security · Week 2026-W45 · 2 November 2026 – 8 November 2026

Israeli incursions dominate as Syria sends advisers to Gulf

Based on 160 reports and articles from 34 organisations.

Share:Download PDFXFacebookTelegramWhatsApp

In short: Reporting this week shows Israeli forces conducting near-daily incursions, shelling and checkpoints across Quneitra and Daraa, while Damascus confirmed sending military advisers to Saudi Arabia and announced foiling a Hezbollah-linked plot against the Homs refinery. The same period saw recurring family and tribal clashes, landmine deaths and continued transitional-justice trials.

Findings

The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights (SOHR) and Syrian outlets including Hawar News, affiliated with the Kurdish-led administration, and ANHA, also linked to that administration, documented 182 Israeli incursions and two killed in southern Syria during October, a figure Enab Baladi, an independent outlet, and Syria TV put at 395 violations overall, calling it the highest monthly toll since the start of 2026. Throughout the week SOHR logged repeated patrols, artillery fire and checkpoints in Quneitra, including incursions into Al-Quneitra village, farmlands near Tel Ahmar Al-Sharqi and a new patrol advance, while Syria TV reported incursions into Ain al-Baida, Ufaniyah, al-Samdaniya and Tal Krum Jaba. The Syrian Observer, an independent outlet, reports Israel is expanding its footprint near water sources, while Enab Baladi analyses three levels of Israeli influence inside Syria. Enab Baladi separately criticises what it calls a 'suspicious silence' from authorities over the incursions.

Syria TV and The Syrian Observer report Syrian security services foiled a Hezbollah-linked plot to bomb the Homs refinery, arresting cell members, after which Hezbollah denied involvement. The story was picked up widely by regional outlets including the pan-Arab, Saudi-owned Asharq Al-Awsat, which notes Syrian media analysis around 'who benefits' naming regime remnants, ISIS or 'rogue states', Dubai-based Sky News Arabia, Russian state news agency TASS, Lebanon's Naharnet and UAE-based The National. Israeli outlet Ynet also covered the foiled plot. The consistent framing across sources is of Syrian intelligence successfully intercepting an external threat, though Asharq al-Awsat's framing leaves open other possible culprits.

Multiple Syrian outlets, including state-run SANA and Qatar-funded [Al Jazeera]-adjacent networks, as well as Enab Baladi and Al-Watan Online, an outlet seen as close to the government, reported on conflicting accounts of Syrian military cooperation with Saudi Arabia. Reuters, cited by North Press (affiliated with the Kurdish-led administration) and Syria TV, said Syrian advisers are training Saudi and Yemeni units, while AFP, cited by Enab Baladi and Syria TV, reported thousands of Syrian soldiers would deploy to Saudi Arabia and Yemen. Damascus denied sending troops to Yemen specifically, per Syria TV and Al-Watan Online, while confirming training cooperation with Riyadh; a Syrian diplomat called talk of deployment 'premature' per Hawar News and ANHA. Middle East Eye frames Syria's potential entry into the Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan defence pact as a bargaining chip vis-a-vis Israel.

SOHR and The Syria Report, an economic think tank, examine shifting security structures: SOHR reports Aleppo residents protesting the presence of pro-government militias known as Shabiha, while The Syria Report finds the private security market being reshaped, with a Sovereign Fund-linked firm absorbing former regime-linked companies. Separately, Syria TV cites a study recommending continued suspension of compulsory conscription until a professional national army is built, and Enab Baladi analyses the shift from factionalism to integration and restructuring of the new army. Syria TV reports the Defense Ministry continues receiving defected officers to settle their status, and the Interior Ministry says it is tightening security vetting of recruits, stating 'no immunity' for those involved in crimes.

Transitional justice developments continued at pace: North Press reports the National Commission for Transitional Justice announced the arrest of a former regime general accused of crimes, while Enab Baladi and Syria TV report on weekly trials, including that of a former deputy military prosecutor accused of killing and torture. SOHR calls for fair trials and legal safeguards after new detainee releases, while Zaman al-Wasl published leaked accounts documenting deaths under torture under the former regime. Syria Direct, an independent outlet, reports Sunni and Alawite residents of Talkalakh are rebuilding fragile coexistence through local security reforms despite unresolved grievances, and The Syrian Observer reports US envoy Tom Barrack met Alawite figures on the coast to discuss rights, security and economic conditions.

Communal and criminal violence recurred in several cities: Hawar News and Syria TV report armed clashes between two families in Hama's al-Sawa'iq neighborhood prompting security intervention, while Syria TV reports a separate family clash in Aleppo killed three and wounded six. Al-Watan Online analyses what it calls multiplying 'tools of chaos' from Hasakah to Sweida disrupting stability, and Al-Watan separately reported a second day of unrest in Hasakah caused by armed groups described as acting outside the law. Hawar News and ANHA report an armed attack on an internal security site in Hasakah countryside.

Landmines and war remnants remained a recurring civilian threat: SOHR reports war remnants continue to pose a deadly threat, and Syria TV, Enab Baladi and SANA each report on fatal blasts, including two girls killed in Deir Ezzor and a landmine from the former regime killing two in Boukamal. Syria TV reports Civil Defense warnings that floods and winter rains could wash mines into previously safe areas, a concern echoed in Syria TV's report on winter redrawing the mine map. Hawar News and ANHA report landmines injured five and killed two security personnel in the same period.

Cross-border incidents and counter-terrorism operations rounded out the picture: SOHR warns against anti-Syrian incitement after a priest's killing in Lebanon and urges faster voluntary returns, while Hawar News and ANHA report a Syrian woman killed and child wounded by Lebanese border guards. North Press and Syria TV report the dismantling of an ISIS cell involved in Aleppo assassinations, and North Press cites CENTCOM directives forming a task force to block Iranian arms transfers by land, which a North Press-quoted analyst says complements the existing naval blockade.

Where they agree

Syrian and regional outlets broadly agree that Israeli incursions into southern Syria have intensified to record levels, with SOHR, Enab Baladi, Hawar News and ANHA all citing similar monthly incursion counts. There is also broad convergence across Syria TV, The Syrian Observer, Asharq Al-Awsat and other regional outlets that Syrian intelligence foiled a Hezbollah-linked plot against the Homs refinery, even as Hezbollah denies involvement. Multiple outlets, including Enab Baladi, SANA and North Press, agree that transitional justice trials and detentions of former regime figures are proceeding on a weekly basis.

Where they differ

Sources disagree on the scale and nature of Syria's military role in Saudi Arabia and Yemen: Reuters-sourced reporting describes training cooperation, AFP-sourced reporting describes a much larger troop deployment, while Damascus and Syrian diplomats deny deployment to Yemen specifically. Enab Baladi's framing of a 'suspicious silence' on Israeli incursions implicitly criticises the government's response, a view not echoed by state-run outlets like SANA, which instead emphasise cooperation achievements such as with Iraq. Views on the Homs refinery plot also diverge, with Asharq al-Awsat noting alternative theories about possible culprits beyond Hezbollah.

Recommendations

  • SOHR recommends that authorities and international actors counter anti-Syrian incitement following the priest's killing in Lebanon and accelerate voluntary refugee returns.
  • SOHR recommends fair trials and legal safeguards for detainees newly released from Syrian prisons.
  • A study cited by Syria TV recommends continued suspension of compulsory military conscription in Syria until a professional national army is built.

Compared with what people said

Syrian discussions focused on daily security more than on the geopolitical files that dominated the media: local criminal incidents, such as the killing of three people in a family clash in Aleppo, the killing of a young man who defended a woman from harassment, and the killing of two brothers in Sheikh Maqsoud, topped the highest engagement and drew the angriest reactions of the week, with repeated calls for execution and disarmament, while the media also covered these incidents but did not give them the same relative weight given to the Israeli incursions and the Saudi Arabia file. The two most prominent stories in the media, namely the Israeli incursions and the Homs refinery plot, intersected with what people were discussing, but with a different tone: while the media described the security response to the Homs plot as an intelligence success, people mixed their anger with demands to close the border with Lebanon and execute those involved, and a minority expressed doubt about the accuracy of the official narrative, considering it a 'distraction.' As for the Israeli incursions, public reaction reflected a normalized frustration at the absence of a government response, which intersects with Enab Baladi's criticism of the 'suspicious silence,' but the media did not cover to the same extent the scattered incidents of civil violence (the tribal clashes in Albu Kamal, the reckless driving incident in Aleppo) which sparked widespread public debate about the weakness of the state and its failure to disarm the community. The media also did not address a security stabbing incident in Amman, which drew a large share of public engagement despite occurring outside Syria, reflecting a gap between media coverage priorities linked to sovereignty and geopolitics, and street priorities linked to personal safety and immediate justice.

To watch

  • Whether Israeli incursions into Quneitra and Daraa continue at the reported pace, given SOHR and Enab Baladi's documentation of record monthly violations and persistent public anger over the lack of government response.
  • Whether further details emerge on the scope of Syrian military cooperation with Saudi Arabia and Yemen, given conflicting Reuters and AFP reports and repeated Syrian denials of troop deployment.
  • Whether the Homs refinery plot investigation produces further arrests or evidence implicating Hezbollah, given denials from the group and public skepticism about the timing of the announcement.
  • Whether transitional justice trials expand beyond Damascus and address public demands for accountability, given Enab Baladi and Syria TV's reporting on weekly hearings and SOHR's calls for fair trials.
  • Whether floods worsen landmine contamination risks as Civil Defense warned, given recurring civilian and child casualties documented by Enab Baladi and Syria TV.
  • Whether communal violence in Hasakah, Hama and Aleppo prompts broader disarmament measures, given repeated family and tribal clashes and public demands reported both in the media and by ordinary Syrians.
