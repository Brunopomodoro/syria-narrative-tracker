The day's stories

  1. Iraqi PM al-Zaidi's historic Damascus visit and $10M shrine donation International relations · People: strongly positive · Outlets: leaning positive · 31% of the discussion
    Iraqi PM Ali al-Zaidi visited Damascus, meeting President al-Sharaa to discuss oil exports via the Baniyas pipeline, trade and security cooperation. He personally donated $10 million to restore the Umayyad Mosque and Sayyida Zainab shrine, a gesture widely covered as historic and symbolically significant for Iraq-Syria ties since Assad's fall.
  2. Flash floods kill several and disrupt life across eastern and northern Syria Economy and services · People: leaning negative · Outlets: mostly neutral · 27% of the discussion
    Heavy rains and flash floods hit Raqqa, Deir ez-Zor, Aleppo, Hasakah and Homs provinces on 29 October, killing five people including four children and leaving five missing, according to Syria's Ministry of Emergency and Disaster Management. Bridges collapsed, roads were cut, IDP camps flooded, and civil defense teams rescued dozens. Officials say 165 field responses were carried out across six provinces.
  3. Fallout from Jordanian airstrikes on Daraa continues Israel and the south · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 14% of the discussion
    Jordanian airstrikes on northern Daraa countryside killed several civilians including children; Syria summoned Jordan's ambassador and formed a joint security committee with Jordan to investigate. Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani toured Daraa, offered condolences, and vowed accountability, while many Syrians online express anger at perceived government inaction and demand a formal Jordanian apology.
  4. Shop closures and layoffs spark debate over Damascus and Aleppo market stagnation Economy and services · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 4% of the discussion
    Reports on shop closures and worker layoffs in Damascus and Aleppo markets drew heavy public commentary blaming high rents, Turkish and Chinese import competition, corruption and poor governance. Commenters compared current hardship unfavorably to, and in some cases nostalgically with, the Assad era.
  5. Kurdish officials say armed groups block displaced Afrin residents' return and seize property North-east and the Kurdish question · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 3% of the discussion
    Kurdish politician Ilham Ahmed said armed groups in Afrin remain the main obstacle to the return of displaced residents, citing seizure of homes, farmland and olive harvests, and illegal taxes. She called on the Syrian government to protect civilians and return seized property to its owners.

Separately, the National Commission for Transitional Justice opened its first recovery center for victims of abuses, in Homs, with international support.

3. What the media said

Floods, Iraq visit and Jordan fallout dominate coverage

In short: Syrian and regional media on 29 October focused on deadly flash floods across several provinces, the historic visit of Iraq's prime minister to Damascus and its economic promise, and the lingering fallout from Jordanian airstrikes on Daraa. Security coverage also noted continuing Israeli incursions into Quneitra and a life sentence for a militiaman linked to Iran's Revolutionary Guard. Ordinary Syrians online echoed the floods and the Iraq visit heavily but reacted far more angrily to the Jordan strikes than most media framing suggested, and also discussed university mergers in the north-east that the wider media barely covered.

Security

Floods dominated security-adjacent coverage, with the Syrian Observatory for Human Rights reporting five dead and five missing as 25 others were rescued, while Enab Baladi and AP separately put the death toll at five in Deir Ezzor and Raqqa, and Al-Araby Al-Jadeed, Al-Modon and Arab News all described floods sweeping six provinces. On the Jordan file, Independent Arabia reported Syria summoned Jordan's ambassador over strikes on the south, Al Jazeera said Damascus and Amman gave contradictory accounts of an attack that killed civilians, and Le Monde said both sides now favour de-escalation; New Lines Magazine investigated the village hit, where Amman said it targeted the Islamic State. Syria TV reported Foreign Minister al-Shaibani visited Daraa, saying the state is the rightful avenger of Syrian blood, a message Al-Watan Online and Zaman al-Wasl also covered as Damascus tries to balance sovereignty with ties to Jordan. On Quneitra, Al-Watan Online and Syria TV reported continued Israeli incursions and a new checkpoint, while The New Arab argued Israel's expanding occupation is deepening Syria's dilemmas. Syria TV and Arab News reported a life sentence for a member of an Iran-linked militia, and the state-run Syrian Observatory for Human Rights reported French fighters accusing Syrian authorities of a blockade and deadly ambush near Harem, a clash also noted by The New Region.

Economy

Coverage centred heavily on the Iraqi prime minister's first official visit to Damascus, with the state-run SANA previewing what it may bring, Al-Watan Online saying Iraq is a gateway for Syrian exports with $5 billion held up by facilitation issues, and Shafaq News and the Qatar-funded Al Jazeera network's sister outlet Al-Araby reporting analysts expect boosted economic and security prospects. SANA also reported Iraq calling for a joint economic council, while Al Arabiya and Syria TV reported Norway's DNO studying exporting Iraqi Kurdish oil through Syria's Baniyas port. Separately, L'Orient Today reported the Syrian lira under pressure with dollars increasingly kept outside banks, and The Syria Report published a central bank statement on inspections of unlicensed exchange firms. Al-Monitor reported US firm HKN aims to double Syria's oil output after taking over Kurdish-run fields amid pollution concerns, and Syria TV described closed shops and halted workshops in Aleppo's commercial sector.

Governance

Governance coverage was dominated by the fallout from Jordanian strikes and Syrian-Iraqi rapprochement, alongside controversies over education and press freedom. Al-Araby Al-Jadeed argued Israel benefits from undermining Damascus-Amman relations, while Al-Araby Al-Jadeed separately reported security understandings forming between Syrian and Iraqi officials on border control. Syria TV reported growing political engagement between Damascus and Baghdad highlighted by the Iraqi premier's visit to the Umayyad Mosque, and the independent Syria TV asked whether the partnership can withstand regional pressures. The Farsi-language BBC Persian reported controversy over Syria's revised textbooks, which critics say add headscarves to images and remove artworks, while Al Akhbar, a Lebanese outlet aligned with the Hezbollah-led camp, examined the removal of Abd al-Rahman al-Kawakibi's name from curricula. Zaman al-Wasl reported Jordan detained a Syrian satirist over alleged insults to Syria, and Zaman al-Wasl separately clarified a German travel warning had been misrepresented, a denial also carried by Syria TV. Asharq Al-Awsat reported Syria's People's Assembly complaining that no draft laws are reaching it from ministries, and New Lines Magazine reported unresolved Assad-era land expropriations near Damascus. Rudaw, affiliated with the Kurdistan Democratic Party, reported the UN human rights chief pledging protection for minorities amid attacks on Kurds.

How they compare

Ordinary Syrians' top story, the Iraqi prime minister's visit, matched the media's heavy coverage and shared its broadly positive tone, though posters were more emotional, with some sarcastic doubts about Iraqi motives that the media did not report. On floods, people's reactions mixed weary resignation and mockery of municipal drainage failures alongside praise for army rescues, a more polarized tone than the mostly factual casualty reporting from outlets like the Syrian Observatory and AP. The Jordan strikes drew the sharpest gap: Syrians were far angrier, demanding apologies, compensation and even prosecution, and mocking the government's measured diplomatic tone, whereas media coverage leaned toward describing diplomatic de-escalation and contradictory accounts. Notably, Syrians discussed two stories the media above barely touched: backlash over merging Rojava and Kobani universities into Euphrates and Aleppo universities (covered briefly by Syria TV and SANA but drawing angry Kurdish-language reactions framing it as erosion of autonomy), and Kurdish officials' complaints that armed groups block displaced Afrin residents from returning and seize their property, which was not reflected among the items reviewed here.
