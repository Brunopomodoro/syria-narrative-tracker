Security

Multiple outlets, including Al Jazeera, Middle East Eye and Arab News, reported protests across Syrian provinces after Israeli forces killed a disabled man near the occupied Golan Heights, with Asharq Al-Awsat and The Jerusalem Post also covering the incident. Syria formally appealed to the UN for protection against Israeli attacks, according to Arab News, while a UN official cited by North Press, an outlet affiliated with the Kurdish-led Autonomous Administration, said Israel carried out unlawful destruction of civilian property. The independent Syrian Observatory for Human Rights reported 37 killed in unexplained arms depot explosions, alongside separate reports on Israeli damage to southern farmland, war remnants killing or injuring 11 children since October, and alleged abuses against Alawite women. State-run SANA and independent Enab Baladi both reported continuing Israeli incursions, detentions and a landmine death in Quneitra. The Sweida-Damascus highway remained closed, with SANA, Arab News and Israeli outlet Ynet all describing the closure by groups variously termed "outlaws" or, per The New Arab, a "National Guard" militia tied to Sheikh al-Hijri. The independent Syrian Dialogue Center analysed a separate security operation in Rankous as a test of the balance between rights and security practice, while Al-Quds al-Arabi reported residents demanding evidence over seven deaths there.

Economy

Economic coverage focused on hardship and reform. SANA, the Syrian state news agency, reported a diesel shortage affecting livelihoods in Aleppo and Idlib, while Syria TV, an independent outlet, described low wages and high rents straining Aleppo residents. Enab Baladi reported the central bank governor's promise that Syria will soon exit the FATF grey list, and the same outlet described a new labour force survey aimed at reintegrating Syria into international statistics. Zaman al-Wasl reported poor Aleppo residents demanding restoration of a displaced market, while Al-Modon questioned whether the sovereign fund's role in Hama amounted to administration or quiet expropriation, a claim Syria TV said the fund denied regarding a separate settlement. Hawar News, affiliated with the Kurdish-led administration, examined the troubled finances of the Agricultural Cooperative Bank, and SANA reported talks on a Saudi-backed food security megaproject.

Governance

Governance coverage centered on the Barrack-al-Hijri dispute and Syria-Israel diplomacy. Enab Baladi, Al-Araby Al-Jadeed, a pan-Arab outlet, and Middle East Eye all reported US envoy Tom Barrack's claim that a "Druze sheikh" misleads people about American backing and that Sweida "lives off" the Captagon trade, while saying a Syria-Israel understanding remains possible; North Press carried a similar account. Al-Hijri responded angrily, saying his movement does not derive legitimacy from Washington, per Enab Baladi. The New Arab examined minority protection as a test for Syria's new political order. Al-Araby Al-Jadeed analysed Syria's shift from revolutionary to constitutional legitimacy, and the same outlet separately described attempts to extradite Bashar and Maher al-Assad as a milestone for transitional justice. SANA reported Syria's accession to the Mecca anti-corruption agreement, and Halab Today said Syria announced it had turned the page on the former regime's legacy regarding nuclear obligations. Locally, Syria TV reported Jobar residents blocking a Damascus ring road to protest reconstruction offers, and Kurdpress reported a Kurdish movement calling for free political party activity.
