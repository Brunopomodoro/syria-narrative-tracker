Media coverage · Daily analysis · 28 October 2026

Daraa Fallout and Iraqi PM's Historic Damascus Visit Dominate

Based on 106 reports and articles from 38 organisations.

Share:XFacebookTelegramWhatsApp

In short: Coverage on 28 October split between the aftermath of Jordan's deadly strikes on Daraa and the first visit by an Iraqi prime minister to Damascus since Assad's fall. The media reported a reopened Nasib border crossing, a joint Syrian-Jordanian investigative committee, and extensive economic and security pledges between Damascus and Baghdad. Ordinary Syrians were far angrier about the Daraa strikes than the media's measured diplomatic framing suggested, while Kurdish-related grievances barely registered in outlet coverage.

Security

The media extensively covered the fallout from Jordanian airstrikes on Daraa that killed civilians, with Asharq Al-Awsat reporting Syria held funerals for victims while Jordan expressed regret, and Al Jazeera, the Qatar-funded broadcaster, reporting the border reopened amid a joint probe. The Syrian Observer said the strikes put Syrian-Jordanian relations to a difficult test, while Foundation for Defense of Democracies argued the strikes exposed a security vacuum in southern Syria, and Asharq Al-Awsat cited a Jordanian source calling areas outside government control a haven for traffickers. Syrian outlets pushed back: Zaman al-Wasl, independent, reported that Jordanian journalists and their government could not provide evidence of alleged "terrorists," and Syria TV, independent, questioned who identified the targets in the Syrian-Jordanian coordination. Enab Baladi, independent, and Hawar News, affiliated with the Kurdish-led administration, both reported formation of a joint investigative committee. Separately, Enab Baladi reported one killed in clashes between Syrian security forces and French fighters in Idlib, and Syria TV and Zaman al-Wasl reported continued Israeli incursions and an abduction in Quneitra.

Economy

The media gave heavy attention to the Iraqi prime minister's visit, with L'Orient-Le Jour reporting economic matters dominated the trip and The New Region saying discussions covered oil exports through Baniyas. Syria TV, state-adjacent independent, announced trade and passenger movement through Nasib had returned to normal, while Al-Watan Online cited an expert saying the dollar trading at 20,000 reflects unclear monetary policy entrenching a cash market. Enab Baladi reported Syria remains burdened by more than 22 billion dollars in inherited debt, and L'Orient-Le Jour said the pound is under pressure with dollars kept out of banks. Hawar News reported farmers in the Jazira region are cutting cultivation due to lack of support and rising prices, while Syria TV examined whether Syria's banking sector could revive via Visa, Mastercard access and a $100 million initiative.

Governance

The Iraqi prime minister's visit dominated governance coverage: The National called it the first high-profile trip since al-Sharaa took power, while Al-Watan Online reported al-Sharaa and al-Zaidi affirmed commitment to cooperation, and Al Akhbar offered a more skeptical analysis of "complicated cooperation." Enab Baladi reported al-Sharaa outlined ties with Iraq covering oil, water and security. The New Arab reported Syria summoned the Jordanian ambassador over the Daraa strikes, and Independent Arabia gave background on Syria's political committees. Syrian Network for Human Rights examined INTERPOL's role in accountability efforts, while Hawar News reported a dialogue meeting in Qamishli on transitional justice, and Syria TV reported a German state official urging expedited protection reviews for 16 Syrians after violent incidents.

Compared with what people said

Ordinary Syrians were far angrier about the Daraa strikes than the media's framing conveyed, with up to 91% of posts expressing overwhelming anger and mockery of Jordanian officials, some accusing Jordan of colluding with Israel, a tone far harsher than the measured diplomatic language in outlets like Asharq Al-Awsat. The Iraqi PM's visit drew milder, mixed reactions online, some welcoming it with hope while others criticized donations as out of touch given poverty, roughly matching the media's largely positive diplomatic framing. Strikingly, Turkish and Kurdish-language backlash over SDF integration with Damascus and attacks on Kurdish families returning to Tal Abyad, both carrying strongly negative tones, received almost no coverage in the items reviewed. Syrians also discussed heavy rains and flash floods in Raqqa, Hasakah and Deir ez-Zor, and reacted to tight Israeli election results, neither of which featured in the media material gathered here.
