Media and experts · Daily brief · 25 October 2026

Israeli escalation in south dominates coverage amid fuel crisis

Based on 95 reports and articles from 28 organisations.

Share:Download PDFXFacebookTelegramWhatsApp

In short: Media and think tanks focused heavily on Israeli incursions, bulldozing and killings in Quneitra and Daraa, with Amnesty International demanding accountability as reported by SANA and Arab News. Education curriculum changes and Syrian-Bahraini diplomacy also drew extensive coverage, while economic reports highlighted diesel shortages and Gulf investment talks. Ordinary Syrians, however, centred their anger on the blocked Suwayda-Damascus highway and worsening fuel shortages, topics that received comparatively less analytical depth from outlets that day.

Security

Multiple outlets reported continued Israeli military activity in southern Syria: SANA said occupation forces advanced into Quneitra and bulldozed farmland, while Syria TV reported digging near Hanouta farm and Zaman al-Wasl documented an incursion into northern Quneitra. Arab News and The New Arab reported a checkpoint being set up and a Daraa officer killed. Rights groups were vocal: Amnesty International called for accountability per SANA, while the Syrian Network for Human Rights demanded an independent probe into a killing west of Daraa, per Syria TV, and Al-Watan Online reported condemnation over the killing of a disabled civilian. Analysts weighed the strategic risk: the Israeli think tank INSS examined unusual protests against Israel from Daraa to Idlib, while Le Monde argued Israel uses 'buffer zones' to extend regional dominance, and Enab Baladi said Israel is managing rather than ending its presence. Separately, SANA reported a highway security officer killed in an armed attack in Daraa countryside, and Iranian state media IRNA reported explosions near Damascus coinciding with Israeli reconnaissance flights.

Economy

Economic coverage focused on diesel shortages and investment talks. Al-Araby Al-Jadeed and Al Arabiya reported the energy ministry addressing a fuel crisis in Aleppo and Idlib, while Enab Baladi detailed urgent measures involving 250 fuel tankers and Al-Modon said poor families await government support. North Press reported rising prices shrinking winter food stocks on the coast, and Al Akhbar said poor-quality gasoline is damaging engines. On investment, SANA and SANA reported Syrian-Saudi talks on economic cooperation, Al-Araby Al-Jadeed reported a rush of Gulf investment, and Syria and Oman discussed bus manufacturing per Arab News and Syria TV. Independent outlet Al-Modon raised questions about the structure and oversight of Syria's sovereign fund, while Zaman al-Wasl reported the fund denying a settlement with Yasar Ibrahim. Enab Baladi said attacks on energy facilities threaten investment with losses worth millions of dollars.

Governance

Governance coverage was dominated by education curriculum controversy and Syrian-Bahraini diplomacy. The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights, a UK-based monitoring group, reported curriculum changes and gender segregation raising concerns, while North Press, Syria TV and Al-Araby Al-Jadeed reported controversy over amended textbooks, and Al-Quds Al-Arabi quoted an official saying the aim is removing Baath content. On diplomacy, SANA, Al-Watan Online and Arab News reported Foreign Minister al-Shaibani's talks with Bahrain on boosting ties and reactivating a joint committee, with Syria TV noting agreement on expanding banking cooperation. Enab Baladi examined why diaspora communities applaud each new ruler, and in an opinion piece Enab Baladi argued Syria needs substantive action, describing the country as 'on the operating table'. Syrian academic Burhan Ghalioun defended criticism as necessary for state-building per Syria TV, while Al-Watan Online reported Syria needs 7,500 judges to fill a judiciary shortfall, and Syria TV said parliament and the justice ministry discussed judicial reform. The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights also reported Damascus home demolitions and education protests in Suwayda.

Compared with what people said

Ordinary Syrians' biggest concern that day, the blocked Suwayda-Damascus highway amid the Barrack-Hijri rift, received only glancing coverage in the professional material, noted mainly by Asharq Al-Awsat on 'outlaw groups' blocking the road and Al Akhbar on Barrack's warning that the Suwayda file is 'heating up', far less attention than people devoted to it with starkly negative, sectarian-tinged reactions. The diesel crisis drew strong public anger, accusations of government lies and hoarding, a tone notably harsher than the more procedural outlet coverage of ministry measures. Israeli incursions were covered extensively by media, and people also expressed mixed alarm and frustration, showing rough alignment there. However, Syrians' debate over a proposed hike in traffic fines, the road accident injuring Qamishli Sport players, and Kurdish demands for constitutional language rights were topics the professional coverage largely did not address that day.
