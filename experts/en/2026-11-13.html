Media coverage · Daily analysis · 13 November 2026

Oil, Diplomacy and Winter Cold Dominate Coverage

Based on 117 reports and articles from 35 organisations.

Share:Download PDFXFacebookTelegramWhatsApp

In short: Media coverage on November 13 centred on Syria's expanding economic ties, from Iraqi oil transiting to Baniyas to renewed foreign representation, while security reporting focused on Israeli incursions in Quneitra and the Mihrac Ural arrest fallout. Governance stories highlighted the reversal of new US/UK visa restrictions after public backlash, alongside protests demanding accountability. Economic reporting also dwelt heavily on the winter heating crisis gripping Syrian households.

Security

Syria TV reported new developments in the arrest of Mihrac Ural (Ali Kayali), noting Lebanon detained a suspect linked to the operation. Several outlets, including Syria TV, Al Jazeera, Asharq Al-Awsat and Naharnet, covered Lebanon's request to Syria to extradite suspects in the killing of former Iraqi MP Ahmed al-Alwani in Beirut. Israeli Defence Minister Katz was quoted by North Press, Syria TV and Asharq Al-Awsat saying Israel does not need opposition or court approval to direct military operations in the region, while Enab Baladi reported he insists on maintaining a 'security zone' in Syria. Enab Baladi and Syria TV reported Israeli forces released two young men in Quneitra while continuing incursions and searches, and SANA said Israeli forces detained three people in Jabata al-Khashab. North Press and Anadolu Agency reported three Syrian army personnel were killed or wounded in an unclaimed attack in Deir ez-Zor, with Enab Baladi giving similar figures. Coverage of a possible Syrian troop deployment to Yemen appeared in Syria TV, RFI, and Anadolu Agency and TRT World, which both cited a Yemeni official denying such reports reflect current reality, while the Turkish state-run Anadolu Agency also reported 52 ceasefire violations on its fourth day.

Economy

Reuters, Syria TV and Enab Baladi reported Iraq now exports about 150,000 barrels of oil daily overland through Syria to Baniyas port as an alternative to the Strait of Hormuz, with TASS and IRNA also covering the route's revival and Syria TV citing a cumulative 1.6 million barrels since the agreement began. Al-Watan Online quoted an academic saying the era of sanctions has ended but the era of financing has not yet begun, stressing trust must come first, while Al-Modon reported growing international momentum to support development and financing in Syria. Heating and fuel shortages dominated Syrian outlets: Hawar News, affiliated with the Kurdish-led administration, reported rising prices narrowing heating options, while Syria TV described firewood, diesel and pistachio shells being used amid a chronic crisis, and SANA and Syria TV reported the start of subsidized heating diesel deliveries. SANA cited the Syrian Electricity Company explaining increased rationing hours, and Syria TV found thermal plants have large capacity but limited output. Independent Arabia reported Syria's citrus sector struggling with weak marketing despite abundant production.

Governance

Multiple outlets, including Al-Monitor, The New Arab and the Turkish state-run Anadolu Agency, reported Syria rolled back new visa restrictions on US and UK citizens after public backlash over fears it would harm tourism; Al-Quds Al-Arabi and Asharq Al-Awsat also covered the reversal. Halab Today and North Press reported Syria regained full membership in the Euro-Mediterranean Postal Union after a 15-year suspension. Syria TV and SANA reported ambassador Hussein al-Sabbagh presented credentials as Syria's permanent delegate to the FAO. SANA and Syria TV reported Qatar and Turkey renewed support for Syria's stability and called for an end to Israeli violations, while Asharq Al-Awsat reported Erdogan asked al-Sharaa about Syria's situation at COP31. Enab Baladi and Enab Baladi reported a demonstration in Aleppo demanding accountability for 'criminals' of the former regime. Enab Baladi and Enab Baladi reported access to information sparking a debate between media and government in Damascus. Al-Modon analysed the persistence of a 'deep state' beyond the fall of regime loyalists, and Al-Modon reported plans to relocate ministries outside Damascus and lease land to Turkey for a hundred years. L'Orient-Le Jour noted Syria's political life still awaits a law on political parties. The Atlantic Council argued Atlantic Council Syria should not join the Mecca Alliance but cooperate with it.

Compared with what people said

Ordinary Syrians' top concerns differed sharply from the media agenda: the largest share of posts, up to 19%, debated a crackdown on 'low-quality content' creators and a proposed morals law, a story largely absent from the media items reviewed. People also mocked the recovery of an Ilyushin aircraft stranded in Libya, a story the media barely touched, and discussed a Damascus car dealership owner accused of fleeing with customers' money, which the media did not cover at all. Where the media and the public overlapped, such as the visa reversal for Americans and the Aleppo accountability protests, Syrians' tone was more mixed and skeptical than the comparatively neutral media framing. The heating crisis, covered extensively by outlets like Syria TV and Hawar News, drew far angrier reactions among ordinary Syrians, who expressed direct anger at the presidency over unresolved shortages. Posts about Israeli detentions and incursions in Quneitra, also covered by Enab Baladi, carried a notably negative public tone, reflecting frustration with both Israeli actions and perceived state inaction.
