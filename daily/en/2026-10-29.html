    Iraqi PM Ali al-Zaidi visited Damascus, meeting President al-Sharaa to discuss oil exports via the Baniyas pipeline, trade and security cooperation. He personally donated $10 million to restore the Umayyad Mosque and Sayyida Zainab shrine, a gesture widely covered as historic and symbolically significant for Iraq-Syria ties since Assad's fall.
  2. Flash floods kill several and disrupt life across eastern and northern Syria Economy and services · People: leaning negative · Outlets: mostly neutral · 27% of the discussion
    Heavy rains and flash floods hit Raqqa, Deir ez-Zor, Aleppo, Hasakah and Homs provinces on 29 October, killing five people including four children and leaving five missing, according to Syria's Ministry of Emergency and Disaster Management. Bridges collapsed, roads were cut, IDP camps flooded, and civil defense teams rescued dozens. Officials say 165 field responses were carried out across six provinces.
  3. Fallout from Jordanian airstrikes on Daraa continues Israel and the south · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 14% of the discussion
    Jordanian airstrikes on northern Daraa countryside killed several civilians including children; Syria summoned Jordan's ambassador and formed a joint security committee with Jordan to investigate. Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani toured Daraa, offered condolences, and vowed accountability, while many Syrians online express anger at perceived government inaction and demand a formal Jordanian apology.
  4. Shop closures and layoffs spark debate over Damascus and Aleppo market stagnation Economy and services · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 4% of the discussion
    Reports on shop closures and worker layoffs in Damascus and Aleppo markets drew heavy public commentary blaming high rents, Turkish and Chinese import competition, corruption and poor governance. Commenters compared current hardship unfavorably to, and in some cases nostalgically with, the Assad era.
  5. Kurdish officials say armed groups block displaced Afrin residents' return and seize property North-east and the Kurdish question · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 3% of the discussion
    Kurdish politician Ilham Ahmed said armed groups in Afrin remain the main obstacle to the return of displaced residents, citing seizure of homes, farmland and olive harvests, and illegal taxes. She called on the Syrian government to protect civilians and return seized property to its owners.
  6. Israeli incursions and shelling continue in Quneitra Israel and the south · People: leaning negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 3% of the discussion
    Israeli forces conducted ground incursions, set up checkpoints, detained a man, and fired artillery shells near Saida al-Golan in southern Quneitra, with UNDOF inspecting the site. Outlets reported 14 incursions, seven detentions and three shelling incidents in Quneitra and Daraa between 12-19 October alone, as the Arab Group at the UN condemned ongoing violations.
  7. Transitional justice: life sentences, mass grave remains, and new recovery center Transitional justice · People: leaning positive · Outlets: mostly neutral · 2% of the discussion
    Syrian courts sentenced former Liwa al-Quds/IRGC-linked militiaman Abdul Jabbar Ghazi Arziq to life in prison, while remains believed to belong to victims of a 2012 massacre were found in Homs. Separately, the National Commission for Transitional Justice opened its first recovery center for victims of abuses, in Homs, with international support.

    Syria agreed on final steps to integrate Kobani and Rojava Universities into the structures of Euphrates and Aleppo universities, sparking objections from Kurdish academics who call it comparable to the former regime's 'Arab Belt' policy. A Rojava University professor said the move threatens recognition of Kurdish-language education.
