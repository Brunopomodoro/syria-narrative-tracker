Frustrated Outlets focused on diplomatic wins (the UN resolution on Syria, Shaibani's Brussels tour, World Bank reconstruction talks) and a new economy minister, while ordinary Syrians kept venting about prices, security killings in Jaramana and Deir ez-Zor, and doubts that any of this diplomacy reaches daily life. Israeli incursions in Quneitra and Daraa drew near-unanimous Arab condemnation from states and officials, but comments show weary resignation rather than hope.   • Shaibani's Brussels Diplomacy and World Bank Reconstruction Talks Foreign Minister Shaibani attended the Schuman Security and Defence Forum and EU-Mediterranean Forum in Brussels, meeting officials including Frederic Hof and UN counterterrorism chief Natalia Gherman, and urged the EU to finalize a partnership agreement. Separately, a World Bank delegation led by Anna Bjerde visited President al-Sharaa and toured Jobar, Damascus, where destruction reaches 93% and reconstruction needs are estimated at $216 billion.
  • New Economy Minister Appointed Amid Debate Over Economic Recovery President al-Sharaa issued decrees appointing Ahmad Rawad Ramadan as Minister of Economy and Foreign Trade, replacing Mohammad Nidal al-Shaar, who was named presidential economic adviser. The move came alongside continued public complaints about the falling Syrian pound, high prices, and a new ban on onion imports until year-end to protect local farmers.
  • Internal Security Officers Killed in Jaramana and Tartus Attacks Two Internal Security Forces members were killed and four wounded in Jaramana, Damascus countryside, on 18 November during a raid to arrest a wanted man after a complaint from his father; the suspect opened fire before being killed. Separately, mourners buried another ISF officer killed earlier in Tartus countryside. Officials called it an isolated law-enforcement incident; the public wants harsher crackdowns.
  • Life Sentence Over Suwayda Killings Sparks Debate on Selective Justice A military criminal court in Damascus sentenced an Internal Security member, Ezzedine Ibrahim, to life in prison for killing five people in Suwayda, a verdict reported across outlets. Many commenters called the sentence too lenient or, conversely, questioned why perpetrators of other Suwayda violence have not faced similar accountability.
  • Israeli Incursions in Quneitra and Daraa Draw Wide Condemnation Israeli forces continued incursions and shelling in Quneitra and Daraa countryside, including the villages of Koya, Samdaniya al-Sharqiya, Buraiqa and Bir Ajam, prompting flares, home searches and some residents to flee. Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Jordan and Egypt condemned the actions; Syria's UN envoy Ibrahim Olabi said the violations must stop and reaffirmed commitment to the 1974 Disengagement Agreement.
  • Standoff Over Rojava University Integration Sparks North-east Protests Students at Rojava University launched a petition and protests demanding official recognition of the university's diploma and admissions, and the preservation of Kurdish-language education and academic independence amid integration talks with Damascus. Kurdish-run outlets and activist accounts framed it as a fight for Kurdish rights; Arabic coverage of the issue was largely absent.
  • Church Leaders Warn of Escalating Attacks on Syrian Christians Syrian church patriarchs warned of a rise in kidnappings and attacks against Christians after armed men assaulted Melkite Greek Catholic Archbishop Jean-Abdo Arbach in Homs, binding and blindfolding him while ransacking his office. International outlets (The Pillar, Euronews, Arab News) carried the warnings, which patriarchs said reflect 'not isolated incidents'.
  • UN Adopts Syria Human Rights Resolution by Consensus for First Time in 15 Years The UN General Assembly's Third Committee adopted a resolution on human rights in Syria by consensus for the first time in 15 years, co-drafted by the US and Qatar. Syria's envoy Ibrahim Olabi called it 'a historic milestone' recognizing government reforms and return of refugees; it also stresses respect for the 1974 Disengagement Agreement.
  • US Offers $5 Million Reward Over Fate of Disappeared Aid Worker Layla Shweikani The US State Department's Rewards for Justice program offered up to $5 million for information on Syrian-American aid worker Layla Shweikani, who disappeared in 2016 after being detained by the former Assad regime along with her father and fiancé. Reports say she was tortured and possibly executed in detention.
  • Family Shooting Kills Eight in Tawamiya Village, Deir ez-Zor A man in Tawamiya village, eastern Deir ez-Zor countryside, shot and killed several family members, including women, before killing himself, in a case authorities said stemmed from a family dispute. The death toll rose to eight after two wounded died later; Internal Security Forces opened an investigation.
