What people are talking about · 13 October 2026, 05:47 UTC

Weary Coverage this cycle centers on a heavy UN Security Council session on Syria's humanitarian situation, continued Israeli incursions in Quneitra/Daraa, Kurdish-Barzani-Abdi talks in Erbil, and persistent fuel shortages despite tanker arrivals. Official and international outlets stress recovery, partnership and sovereignty, while ordinary Syrians online voice fatigue over fuel/property issues, mockery of corruption, and divided reactions to Kurdish integration and the Hasakah weapons crackdown. Regional stories like Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and Lebanon's explosives seizure also draw strong public reaction. Nostalgia and pride about Syrian heritage and tourism content coexist with anger over economic hardship and expropriation law.

  • Israeli Incursions Continue in Quneitra and Daraa Outlets report near-daily Israeli military incursions, detentions, demolitions and gunfire across southern Syria villages, alongside a UN Security Council briefing where Syria's envoy and multiple states condemned the actions and demanded withdrawal under the 1974 agreement.
  • Barzani-Abdi Meetings Advance Talks on Kurdish Constitutional Rights Nechirvan Barzani and Masoud Barzani hosted Syrian presidential adviser Mazloum Abdi in Erbil during the MERI Forum, with both sides stressing the importance of enshrining Kurdish rights in Syria's constitution and praising SDF integration into the Syrian army as a major step, though concerns remain over implementation.
  • Public Backlash Over Property Expropriation Law A TV debate on whether Syria's expropriation law will be repealed drew heavy public engagement, with citizens demanding compensation reform, criticizing officials' responses, and accusing the law of enabling land seizures that favor investors over original owners.
  • Diesel Shortages Persist Despite Tanker Arrivals and Calls to Sack Petroleum Chief State and independent media report new diesel/gasoline tanker arrivals at Baniyas with rejections of two tankers for non-compliance, as Aleppo suffers acute diesel shortages halting transport; an MP publicly called for the petroleum company director's dismissal amid black-market diesel reports.
  • Houthi Attacks on Saudi Arabia Disrupt Flights, Kill Syrian Among Victims Houthi ballistic missile attacks on Riyadh and Khamis Mushait prompted interceptions by the Saudi-led coalition, flight suspensions by Qatar Airways and Etihad, and mourning for a Syrian woman killed at King Khalid International Airport, whose funeral was held in Riyadh.
  • Transitional Justice Court Continues Trial of War Crimes Defendants Damascus's Fourth Criminal Court held 19 sessions trying defendants accused of war crimes and crimes against humanity tied to the former regime, as officials and UN envoy Cordone stressed Syrian ownership of transitional justice with international cooperation offered.
  • Lebanese Army Seizes Explosives Smuggled from Syria Lebanon's army seized around 200kg of explosives intended for smuggling from Syria near Qasr-Hermel, with smugglers fleeing back across the border; the incident follows a similar seizure on October 1 and comes amid PM Nawaf Salam's pledge to confront illegal weapons.
  • Hasakah Weapons Ban and Arab-Kurdish Tensions Hasakah's internal security directorate imposed a strict weapons ban in the city following recent unrest, while MP Abdulhakim Bashar criticized selective solidarity with victims, and reports surfaced of attacks on Kurdish journalists and Radio Rojava staff amid integration tensions.
  • Seventh Anniversary of Hevrin Khalaf Assassination Kurdish outlets and public commenters marked the seventh anniversary of the assassination of Kurdish politician Hevrin Khalaf, with tributes describing her as a symbol of peace and women's resistance, while some posts identify the Ahrar al-Sharqiya militia, now part of the Syrian army, as responsible.
  • Dust Storm Causes Choking Cases and Traffic Disruption in East Syria A severe dust storm hit Raqqa, Deir ez-Zor and Hasakah, causing 271 hospital visits including 63 choking cases, flooding and power outages, as civil defense and internal security forces deployed to manage traffic and warn residents.
