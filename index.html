What people are talking about · 13 October 2026, 00:42 UTC

Frustrated UN Security Council coverage of Syria dominated outlet reporting, with officials and diplomats emphasizing recovery, sovereignty and Israeli violations in the south, while public commentary focused heavily on continued Israeli incursions in Quneitra and Daraa, mockery of government rhetoric about Yemen while Syrian land remains occupied, fuel and diesel shortages, and anger over property expropriation laws. Kurdish-language and Kurdish-outlet audiences discussed the Barzani-Abdi talks and Hasakah tensions with cautious hope mixed with concern over incomplete Kurdish-language education and security incidents. Economic anxiety over the falling lira, customs changes, and real estate prices was widespread, alongside satirical and angry comments about corruption. Entertainment, travel and cultural content drew warm, often nostalgic reactions from diaspora and foreign viewers.

  • Israeli Incursions Continue in Quneitra and Daraa Outlets extensively reported daily Israeli military activity in southern Syria including raids, detentions, demolitions and a UN Security Council session where officials condemned the incursions and called for Israeli withdrawal under the 1974 disengagement agreement. Public anger focused on the contrast between government rhetoric about sending forces to Yemen while failing to confront Israeli occupation at home.
  • Barzani-Abdi Meetings Advance Talks on Kurdish Constitutional Rights Nechirvan Barzani and Masoud Barzani hosted Mazloum Abdi in Erbil for talks on Kurdish rights, constitutional guarantees, and integration of SDF fighters into the Syrian army, amid the MERI Forum where Ilham Ahmad flagged gaps in Kurdish-language education and women's participation.
  • Public Backlash Over Property Expropriation Law A televised debate on Syria's property expropriation law sparked intense public criticism, with commenters citing historical cases of unfair compensation under the former regime and accusing the current government of perpetuating the same practices to benefit investors over citizens.
  • Houthi Attacks on Saudi Arabia Kill Syrian Among Victims Houthi ballistic missile attacks targeting Riyadh and Khamis Mushait were intercepted by the Saudi-led coalition, with disruptions at King Khalid International Airport; a Syrian widow was reported killed, and public debate emerged over whether Syria should send troops to support Saudi Arabia against the Houthis.
  • Diesel Shortages Persist Despite Tanker Arrivals and Calls to Sack Petroleum Chief State media and independent outlets reported new diesel shipments arriving at Baniyas port and new pricing, while commenters report ongoing black market shortages and renewed calls from parliament members for the oil company director to be fired over mismanagement.
  • Lebanese Army Seizes Explosives Smuggled from Syria The Lebanese army announced seizing around 200kg of explosives near the Syria-Lebanon border intended for smuggling, with smugglers fleeing into Syria; Lebanese PM Nawaf Salam vowed to confront illegal weapons firmly, amid speculation linking the incident to Hezbollah.
  • Hasakah Weapons Ban and Arab-Kurdish Tensions Internal security in Hasakah announced a strict weapons ban, amid reports of abductions and assaults attributed to Revolutionary Youth groups affiliated with SDF, raising concerns about integration of SDF-linked formations, and debate in the People's Assembly over selective solidarity with victims.
  • Seventh Anniversary of Hevrin Khalaf Assassination Kurdish outlets and public voices marked seven years since the assassination of Kurdish politician Hevrin Khalaf, with tributes describing her as a symbol of peace and women's resistance, while some commenters attributed her killing to factions now part of the Syrian state.
  • Transitional Justice Court Continues Trial of War Crimes Defendants The Fourth Criminal Court for transitional justice in Damascus held 19 sessions on war crimes and crimes against humanity linked to the former regime, with officials stressing no perpetrator will escape punishment while a UN deputy envoy pledged support respecting Syrian ownership of the process.
