What people are talking about · 11 October 2026, 18:40 UTC

Frustrated Official media focused heavily on tech investment, diplomacy and solidarity with Saudi Arabia after the Houthi airport attack, while ordinary Syrians online were preoccupied with the collapsing lira, fuel quality scandals, and daily economic hardship. Public anger also surfaced over the removal of the Saladin statue, university gender restrictions, and unresolved Kurdish-Arab tensions in Hasakah. Reactions to Israeli incursions in the south and the UK's deportation decision were largely resigned or angry, with little faith expressed in government solutions.

  • Currency Collapse, Fuel Quality Crisis and Fiscal Confusion The Syrian lira continued to slide past 140 to the dollar, with public anger over fuel adulteration damaging vehicles, denials from the Finance Ministry over sovereign fund figures and new fees, and disputes over tax claims. Officials denied rumors while citizens reported real hardship with heating fuel and prices.
  • Arab-Kurdish Tensions and Old-Currency Confusion in Hasakah Confusion over a rumored deadline to stop accepting the old Syrian currency sparked panic and dollar demand in Hasakah, denied by local officials, while deeper Arab-Kurdish tensions persisted following earlier protests, with incidents including a lawyers' syndicate dispute, vandalism of a martyrs' council, and online debate over integration and Kurdish rights.
  • Removal of Saladin Statue in Damascus Sparks Controversy Authorities relocated the Saladin statue from near Damascus Citadel for restoration work, but the move triggered public suspicion and conspiracy theories linking it to Israeli influence, alongside unrelated reports of an Israeli telecom signal appearing near Jdeidat Artouz.
  • Houthi Attack on Riyadh's King Khalid Airport Kills 12, Including a Syrian A Houthi strike on King Khalid International Airport killed at least 12 people including a Syrian national and wounded over 300. Syrian officials expressed solidarity with Saudi Arabia, and regional/international bodies condemned the attack, which continued with repeated strikes over the following days.
  • Israeli Incursions and Detentions Continue in Southern Syria Israeli forces carried out multiple incursions into Quneitra and Daraa countryside in a single day, detaining and releasing shepherds, blocking farmers, and conducting house searches, alongside continued overflights. The UN reported dozens of Syrians still detained and hundreds of incursions in September.
  • Tech Sector Push and Meta/Microsoft Reengagement President al-Sharaa met diaspora tech delegations including Microsoft and LigaData representatives, while the Communications Ministry negotiated with Meta to restore commercial services to Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, framed as building Syria's digital future and attracting investment.
  • UK Revokes Syrian Surgeon's Refugee Status Amid Deportation Debate The UK Home Office revoked a Syrian surgeon's refugee status, moving toward deportation despite UNHCR warnings against returns before Syria's stability improves; the decision drew criticism from British commentators citing ongoing Israeli strikes, instability and lack of UK embassy presence.
  • Protests Over Dangerous Damascus-Baghdad Road Through Dmeir Residents of Dmeir blocked the Damascus-Baghdad highway with burning tires, protesting the rerouting of truck traffic through their town, citing road safety fears especially with the start of the school year; local officials met with residents to discuss demands.
  • Backlash Over Gender-Restricted University Programs The Ministry of Higher Education's decision to restrict certain faculties (such as culinary and child-rearing) to female students only, and other specialties to males, sparked criticism and satire, with officials defending it as a longstanding rather than new restriction.
  • Arrest of Former Republican Guard General 'Guardian of the Capital' Syrian internal security forces arrested a former Republican Guards brigadier general linked to Bashar al-Assad, known as the 'Guardian of the Capital,' as part of continuing efforts to pursue former regime figures.
