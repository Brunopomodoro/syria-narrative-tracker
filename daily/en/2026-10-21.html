  1. Sarout chants controversy ignites nationwide anger and sectarian blame Society and daily life · People: strongly negative · Outlets: leaning negative · 23% of the discussion

Public reactions split along regional lines, with Deir ez-Zor residents accused of the chants facing backlash.

Security

Syrian outlets reported continued Israeli shelling around Tal al-Ahmar al-Sharqi in Quneitra countryside, with SANA, Syria TV, Zaman al-Wasl and regional outlets including Al Arabiya and Asharq Al-Awsat all noting the incident, while SANA and Hawar News, a Kurdish-administration-affiliated outlet, also reported shelling near Abidin in western Daraa. Syria TV examined Israel's motives for expanding its presence in the south, citing thousands of violations since the year began, while Al Araby al-Jadeed, Qatar-funded, documented testimonies from southern residents on the toll of incursions. The pro-Israel Foundation for Defense of Democracies separately argued Turkey's stated justification for its military presence in Syria does not hold up. Syria TV reported Turkish President Erdogan congratulated Syrian and Turkish intelligence on the arrest of Meraj Ural (Ali Kayali), also covered by Al Majalla, while Al-Watan Online cited an academic saying Israel's actions breach international law. Other items noted an unexplained fire at an oil facility in Hasakah countryside (Hawar News), the detention of six former regime members in Hama over civilian violations (Syria TV), and Lebanon's army detaining 186 Syrians for illegal entry (Syria TV).

Economy

SANA reported the government is advancing fiscal reforms toward clearer public spending and tax systems (SANA), while Zaman al-Wasl reported the state recovered 80% of businessman Sobhi Abbas's fuel assets in a settlement (Zaman al-Wasl) and separately that the deputy head of the Syrian Petroleum Company had assets frozen and a travel ban imposed (Zaman al-Wasl, Zaman al-Wasl), part of what Zaman al-Wasl described as a broad presidential audit of the oil sector (Zaman al-Wasl). The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights, a UK-based monitor, reported a fire at the Al-Jabsa oil facility amid the fuel crisis (Syrian Observatory for Human Rights). Enab Baladi, an independent outlet, reported JPMorgan is helping Syria secure credit to purchase fuel (Enab Baladi) and that the Agricultural Bank mobilized branches to settle wheat payments to farmers (Enab Baladi). Other coverage noted Syrian factories under pressure from imports (Syria TV), an Aleppo diesel shortage disrupting transport and electricity (Syria TV), groundwater depletion in Idlib from unregulated wells and drought (Syria TV), and an expert cited by Al-Watan Online saying central bank platforms cannot curb exchange rates while cash dominates (Al-Watan Online).

Governance

Multiple Syrian outlets, state-run SANA among them, reported President al-Sharaa met US envoy Tom Barrack in Damascus with Foreign Minister al-Shaibani present, discussing regional developments, with Barrack quoted calling Syria's role pivotal to lasting peace (SANA, Syria TV, Al-Watan Online). Turkish President Erdogan said Turkey continues to support Syria's stability and confirmed an upcoming visit, his first since the fall of the Assad regime, according to the Turkish state-affiliated Daily Sabah (Daily Sabah, Al-Watan Online). Parliament repealed a law allowing the Education Ministry to seize property, reported by both state-run SANA and independent Zaman al-Wasl (SANA, Zaman al-Wasl), while the Syrian Observer said the People's Assembly is advancing wider legislative reforms on employment, penal, property and national security law (The Syrian Observer). Enab Baladi reported foreign ministry official Qutaiba Idlibi resigned (Enab Baladi). Kurdish-affiliated Hawar News reported a Kurdish-led initiative seeking constitutional recognition of the Kurdish language (Hawar News), and quoted Kurdish National Council official Qere Yilan on the need for political struggle to protect Kurdish rights (Hawar News). Qatar-funded Al Araby al-Jadeed warned public trust in Syria's institutions is eroding even before any state collapse (Al Araby al-Jadeed). SANA also reported Justice Minister Mazhar al-Weis stated that insulting the name of Abdul Basit al-Sarout is a moral crime (SANA), and Al-Modon reported a committee was formed to investigate alleged abuses related to Sarout (Al-Modon).
